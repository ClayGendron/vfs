# The storage seam's protocol families follow the kernel's operation groups, the write family no longer carries the edges or the trash, and the old family names are gone before the surface freezes

A draft, written on 2026-11-25. It is not reviewed, and nothing is built. It was checked against `dev` at `27f3d24d`. It serves criterion 4 of roadmap story K9 (`context/standards/roadmap.md:220`), the frozen list of storage protocols, at gate b. It is converted from [the archived spec 179](../../context/specifications/archive/179-the-storage-seam-as-the-kernel/spec.md), section 1.2 and task 2 of [its task list](../../context/specifications/archive/179-the-storage-seam-as-the-kernel/tasks.md), called spec 179 below, read through [the study of that source](../2026-11-25-the-backlog-converted/studies/165-181/179-the-storage-seam-as-the-kernel.md) and [the reassessment against dev](../2026-11-25-the-backlog-converted/reassessment/seam.md), section 179-2. It rests on roadmap rows D2 (the freeze rule), D4 and D5 (decided), D63 (a lean), and on unit [181-1](../2026-11-25-every-routed-op-sits-in-one-group-and-every-face-lists-the-verbs-in-that-order/spec.md), which writes the groups. Its build waits for one ruling, the timing of the regroup (Choices to confirm, 1).

## The outcome wanted

A storage author reads `vfs.storage.protocol` top to bottom in the order of the kernel's groups and finds one small protocol per family: a backend that serves reads and plain writes implements `SupportsFileRead` and `SupportsFileWrite` and nothing else, and is not made to write edges, a trash or copies to claim `write`. The write family holds `write`, `mkdir`, `delete` and `move`, and no edge verb and no trash verb. `tree` is no longer part of the minimum a mount requires: a backend without it mounts and is refused `tree` with `unsupported`, as a backend without `glob` is refused `glob` today. `SupportsRead` and `SupportsMutation` exist nowhere in `src/` or `tests/`, so the list K9 freezes names the families as they will stay. A test in `tests/storage/test_protocol.py` fails by name when a family straddles two groups, when an op is in no family or in two, or when an old name comes back. No verb's arguments, rows, errors or statements change for a backend that implements every family, which is every backend `vfs` ships.

## Terms

- **Family.** One `Supports*` protocol whose methods are routed ops. A backend implements a family whole or not at all, and `storage_ops` derives the family's ops from it.
- **Group.** One value of 181-1's `OpGroup`: what kind of fact an op touches.
- **Minimum.** What `StorageBackend` requires of every mounted storage: a family's methods plus `name`, `description` and `capabilities()`.
- **Gate axis.** `MUTATING_OPS` and `WRITE_GATED_OPS` in `vfs.ops`, which decide which ops a read-only mount refuses. It is a second axis, independent of the families.

## The problem

The seam's families were drawn along the write gate, so the one write family carries ten verbs of four kinds: file writes, a copy the kernel can derive, the trash and the edge projection. A backend that can store files but keeps no trash and no graph, which is the backend 179-10 is meant to serve, cannot claim `write` without stubbing six verbs it does not have, and a stub is a capability that lies, which the protocol module's own rule forbids ("self-declaration, never structural sniffing", `protocol.py:7-10`). The minimum also requires `tree`, a verb the kernel can build from `ls`. Story K9 freezes the storage protocols at gate b, and after the freeze no family may be renamed, split or removed (`release.md:14`). So this is the last point at which the families can be drawn along the kernel's groups; after it, 179-10 could derive a verb only for a backend that already implements a ten-verb family. Nothing here runs per call beyond an `isinstance` check that exists today, so the cost is to backend authors and to the freeze, not to the reference deployment.

## Decisions

1. **Each family lies inside one group of `OP_GROUPS`, and every routed op lies in exactly one family.** Reason: spec 179 section 1.2's rule, "Families follow the kernel groups, not the write gate", which 181-1's table makes testable. A family is the unit a backend implements whole; a group is how a reader finds it. A group may hold several families, so that a backend can implement part of a group: the `query` group holds four, because a backend routinely has `glob` and `grep` without a retrieval index (`protocol.py:291-294`). Precedent: `SupportsPatternSearch` and `SupportsRankedSearch` were split on exactly that ground.
2. **The families, in this order, with these methods:**
   - `filesystem`: `SupportsFileRead` (`stat`, `ls`, `read`), `SupportsFileWrite` (`write`, `mkdir`, `delete`, `move`), `SupportsCopy` (`copy`).
   - `query`: `SupportsPatternSearch` (`glob`, `grep`), `SupportsRankedSearch` (`search`), `SupportsTraversal` (`traverse`), `SupportsEdges` (`mkedge`, `rmedge`).
   - `history`: `SupportsTrash` (`restore`, `destroy`).
   - `rights`: `SupportsGrants` (`grant`, `revoke`, `grants`, `set_posture`, `add_member`, `remove_member`).
   - `derived`: `SupportsTree` (`tree`), `SupportsEdit` (`edit`).
   - `execution`: `SupportsRun` (`run`).

   Reason: spec 179 section 1.2's table, with four corrections against `dev`. `SupportsGlean` and `SupportsGraph` are `SupportsRankedSearch` and `SupportsTraversal`, the names `dev` already uses (`protocol.py:340`, `:370`; the old names are pinned gone by `tests/storage/test_protocol.py::test_the_old_family_names_are_gone`). `SupportsRights` is not made (decision 4). `SupportsRun` is kept (decision 5). `SupportsFeatures` is not added here, because `features()` is 179-10's (spec 179 section 2). `copy` and `edit` are families of their own because the kernel can derive them (spec 179 section 4.2), so a backend that lacks them must still be able to claim `write`. Inside a group the families follow 181-1's surface order, so reading the families in order reads `SURFACE_ORDER`. A later family joins its group at the place 181-1's table gives its verb: in `history`, `SupportsHistory` (`versions`, unit 180-1) and `SupportsAcceptBody` (`accept_body`, unit 175-3) come before `SupportsTrash`, in that order (181-1's decision 14), and the drift test's count follows `ALL_OPS`, 25 ops in 12 families on `dev`.
3. **A family keeps its name when its methods do not change, and takes a new name when they do; the old name is deleted, never aliased.** `SupportsRead` loses `tree`, so it becomes `SupportsFileRead`. `SupportsMutation` is split five ways, so it is deleted. `SupportsPatternSearch`, `SupportsRankedSearch`, `SupportsTraversal`, `SupportsRun` and `SupportsGrants` keep their names and their methods. Reason: a name that stays while its members change is a change of meaning the freeze rule exists to stop, and a name that changes while its members stay is churn for every backend author. Precedent: AGENTS.md's pre-release rule ("no legacy to protect"), and `test_the_old_index_names_are_gone` (`tests/storage/test_protocol.py:235`), which pinned the last rename of a family the same way.
4. **The rights family stays `SupportsGrants`.** Spec 179 renamed it `SupportsRights` to match its group. Its methods are exactly `GRANT_OPS` today and after (`ops.py:91`), so decision 3 keeps the name, and decision 4 of the kernel rulings says a group's name is documentation, not a public name. Choices to confirm, 3.
5. **`SupportsRun` stays as it is, in the `execution` group.** Reason: `run` is a live op on `dev` (`ops.py:89`, `EXEC_OPS`), and D63 leans to keeping it, so unit 167-1 is moot and no unit retires `run`. Were the lean ruled the other way before the freeze, the change that removes the verb would delete `SupportsRun` and one row of `_FAMILY_OPS`. Choices to confirm, 2.
6. **`tree` leaves the minimum: `StorageBackend` becomes `SupportsFileRead` plus `name`, `description` and `capabilities()`.** `_call_storage`'s `tree` arm gains the family check every other optional family has, so a storage that declares `tree` without the method is answered `unsupported`, never an `AttributeError`. Until 179-10 derives `tree`, a mount whose storage lacks it is refused `tree` at the gate, with zero storage calls, and is skipped by a `tree` over a parent, as a storage that does not declare `tree` is skipped today (`listing.py:308`). Reason: spec 179 section 1.2 ("The minimum shrinks from four methods to three"). It is done here and not in 179-10 because 179-10 is gate f, after the freeze: shrinking `StorageBackend` then would take `tree` away from every caller typed against it, a change of meaning the freeze forbids. Every backend `vfs` ships implements `SupportsTree`, so none of them changes. Choices to confirm, 4.
7. **`SupportsHolds`, `SupportsTraits`, `SupportsRebuild`, `SupportsClose` and, once the kernel-records stack lands, `SupportsRecordPlace` are not families and keep their names and their methods.** None has a row in `_FAMILY_OPS`, as today. `SupportsHolds` sits under the rights banner after `SupportsGrants`, because it answers the rights question for a mount change (`mounts.py:355-374`). It is not folded into `SupportsGrants`, because `rights_refusal` relies on a storage that keeps grants and cannot answer `holds` being refused `unsupported` (`mounts.py:371-373`, pinned by `tests/base/test_mount_authority.py::test_a_storage_that_keeps_grants_and_cannot_answer_refuses_a_named_caller`); folded, such a storage would lose its grant ops. The other four sit under one banner, `Maintenance protocols, never routed`, after `StorageBackend`. Reason: the stack's own rule for `SupportsRecordPlace` ("It is not a capability, because no verb reaches a record"), which holds for all five.
8. **Every method moves with its signature, defaults and docstring unchanged.** The family docstrings are rewritten to say what each family holds, and the sentence of `protocol.py:186-191` that ties the family boundary to the write gate is deleted. The paging paragraph of `SupportsRead` (`protocol.py:132-141`) moves to `SupportsFileRead` and names `SupportsTree` for `tree`. `MUTATING_OPS`, `WRITE_GATED_OPS` and every other dispatch class in `vfs.ops` are unchanged: the write gate is its own axis, and the five families of decision 2 that write (`SupportsFileWrite`, `SupportsCopy`, `SupportsEdges`, `SupportsTrash`, `SupportsEdit`) hold exactly `MUTATING_OPS` between them on `dev`; a later family whose op joins `MUTATING_OPS`, as `SupportsAcceptBody` does, joins that union (decision 14). Reason: a regroup that also changed a signature could not be reviewed as moves, and the gate's meaning is not this spec's to change. Precedent: spec 179 section 1.2 ("`MUTATING_OPS` stays the write gate's set").

## Cost

No statement, lock, loop or stored structure is added on any engine, so the per-write, per-index-visit, per-batch, per-corpus and per-lock multipliers of the reference deployment are untouched. Per verb call, about 150,000 a day, `_call_storage` runs one `isinstance` against a runtime-checkable protocol, as it does today; the check visits each of the protocol's members, so a write's check falls from the ten members of `SupportsMutation` to the four of `SupportsFileWrite`, and `tree` gains a check of one member. No call does I/O for it. `storage_ops` walks twelve families in place of seven, once per `capabilities()` call of a backend that derives its set, which is once per mount. Nothing grows with a batch, a corpus or an engine's caps, so no budget is involved.
