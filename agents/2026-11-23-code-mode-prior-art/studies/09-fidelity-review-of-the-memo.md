# 09 — Fidelity review of the code-mode memo against its studies

This review checks the memo `2026-11-23-code-mode-keeps-the-tool-catalog-out-of-the-context-and-vfs-can-add-search-and-authority-to-it.md` against the brief and the eight studies in this folder (`00-brief.md`, `01` to `08`) and the experiment outputs under `experiments/`, on five axes: every number against the study it cites, with the same workload and measurer; labels (EXECUTED, READ, INFERRED, search excerpt); claims absent from the studies; whether sections 8 and 9 contradict a study or drop its caveats; and the shape and voice rules of `context/research/README.md` ("The memo", "The rules") and `AGENTS.md` (the `agents/` bullet). Memo lines are cited as `memo:N`, study lines as `NN:N`. The memo was not edited.

## High

H1. The answer overstates the convergence. Memo "The answer" (`memo:9`): "Cloudflare, Anthropic, OpenAI, Codex and nine open-source projects all converged on that, plus a ranked search over tool text that returns about five tools, plus credentials kept on the host." The studies say otherwise on all three counts. Ranked search: five of nine open-source projects rank; mcp-use substring-matches and goose, lootbox and mcporter only list (`03:172`); Cloudflare's production server has no index at all, the model's code scans the 27 MB spec (`01:79`), and its in-sandbox ranker returns up to 50 (`01:75`). Result counts: Codex defaults to 8 (`02:101`), UTCP 10 and pctx 10 (`03:40`, `03:106`), Anthropic 5 (`02:45`). Credentials: mcporter, the UTCP CLI and lootbox leave tokens readable by the agent's shell or run tools with `--allow-all` (`03:173`). Not shown the catalog: `createCodeTool` splices every declaration into its one tool (`01:55`), Codex shows rendered declarations up front (`02:116`), smolagents lists every tool (`04:87`). The memo's own section 1 says the first of these (`memo:32`). Fix: "The designs built for large catalogs (Cloudflare's runtime and API server, Anthropic's tool search and programmatic calling, OpenAI's and Codex's deferred tools, and most of the nine open-source projects) converged on that: a search over tool text that returns a shortlist (5 at Anthropic, 8 at Codex, 10 at UTCP and pctx, up to 50 in Cloudflare's runtime; five of nine open-source projects rank, the rest list or substring-match), and credentials kept on the host in all but the CLI-shaped designs (mcporter, the UTCP CLI, lootbox)."

H2. Rights-filtered discovery is claimed as unique while three studied designs do it. Memo section 8, "Where vfs can be the better choice" (`memo:136`): "Anthropic searches whatever the request carries, Codex whatever is registered, and the registry that does filter does so after ranking." Study 03 reads executor's search as BM25 whose statistics "count the rows the scopes permit", ranking and page in one statement, and calls it "the shape vfs already has" (`03:112`); FastMCP's catalog "is request-scoped — different users may see different tools based on auth" (`03:144`); Cloudflare's Zero Trust portal shows a server only to users who match its Allow policy (`01:81`, `01:134`). The sentence keeps only the one post-filter case (`06:87`). The synthesis claim survives only as the combination of three properties, which is how study 03 states it (`03:191`). Fix: "Anthropic searches whatever the request carries and Codex whatever is registered; the agentic-community registry filters after ranking; executor ranks in Postgres over the caller's permitted rows, FastMCP scopes its catalog by auth, and Cloudflare's portal lists only the servers a user's Allow policy admits. vfs's rights filtering is therefore not unique on its own; what is unique is the combination below."

H3. The CodeAct count describes a different comparison. Memo section 3, table (`memo:67`): "CodeAct: code beats JSON-in-text for 12 of 18 models". Study 04 gives the notebook's tally as "code has the highest success for 12 models, JSON for 4 and text for 2" across three formats (`04:79`); its table (`04:60-77`) shows code strictly above JSON for 10 models, tied for 3 and below for 5. Fix: "CodeAct: code has the highest success of the three formats for 12 of 18 models (strictly above JSON for 10, tied for 3, below for 5)".

H4. One row attributes three measurers' figures to Bifrost. Memo section 3, table (`memo:68`): "input tokens down 58 to 93% once the catalog is large; more tokens at about 10 tools; output tokens up to 2.2× | a gateway's Starlark code mode | Bifrost (vendor)". In study 04 Bifrost measured input tokens −58.2/−84.5/−92.8% at 96/251/508 tools and output tokens −3.7/+35.3/+72.2% (`04:152`); "more tokens at about 10 tools" is the scripted FastMCP #4902 comment, with no model, 4.0k against 5.3k (`04:155`); "output tokens 2.2× higher" is AIMultiple, from a search summary (`04:153`). The row also drops Bifrost's own caveat that one R3 query (14.3M → 245k tokens) drives much of the largest reduction (`04:152`). Fix: split into three rows: "input tokens −58 to −93% over 96 to 508 tools, much of the top figure from one query; output tokens −4% to +72% | Bifrost (vendor), claude-sonnet-4-6, Starlark"; "code mode costs more input tokens at 10 tools, breaks even near 10 to 20 | FastMCP #4902 comment, scripted policy, no model"; "output tokens 2.2× | AIMultiple, Bright Data server, search summary".

H5. "No source compares a shell" contradicts the memo's own table. Memo section 3 (`memo:73`): "Third, no source anywhere compares a shell-only surface against Python or JavaScript code mode on the same tasks"; repeated in section 10 (`memo:161`): "No source measures a shell-only surface against code mode." The table two lines above (`memo:70`) reports "Devil", Python CodeAct against a bash-only agent on the same repository tasks, and "Is Bash All You Need?", bash against programmatic calling (`04:158-159`). What study 04 says is unmeasured is narrower: "No measurement exists of a pipe-and-text shell without an interpreter against Python code mode. Every bash-only result had Python, `jq` and `awk` available" (`04:280`, `04:224`). Fix, in both places: "no source compares a pipe-and-text shell with no interpreter reachable (no Python, `jq` or `awk`) against code mode on the same tasks; every bash-only win had an interpreter one keystroke away."

H6. The cost of the `cli` description is understated by about fifty times. Memo section 9, item 3 (`memo:146`): "The `cli` description plus one hint line, "tools live under `/.vfs/mcp` and `/.vfs/tools`; `ls` them or `search` them", costs a dozen tokens". Study 02 prices only the hint line at a dozen tokens (`02:132`); study 07 gives the drafted `cli` description as about 2,300 characters (`07:56`), about 575 estimated tokens at chars/4. Fix: "One hint line added to the `cli` description ... costs about a dozen tokens on top of the description itself (about 2,300 characters, roughly 575 estimated tokens, study 07 F5); `ls /.vfs/mcp` about 100."

H7. The search-over-grep margin is stated for one corpus as if for both. Memo section 9, item 1 (`memo:144`): "vfs lexical search beat key-word `grep` at hit@1 by 0.15 to 0.25 on the same files". The range holds on the combined corpus (0.477 − 0.323 and 0.492 − 0.246, `08:142-151`) but not on the GitHub-only corpus, where the gaps are 0.239 (V1), 0.326 (V2) and 0.457 (V3) (`08:157-169`). The memo copied study 08's synthesis sentence, which cites both tables (`08:204`). The error understates vfs's margin. Fix: "by 0.15 to 0.25 on the combined 1,107-tool corpus and 0.24 to 0.46 on GitHub's 149 tools".

## Medium

M1. Search-excerpt figures sit in sections labelled READ without saying so. The memo's Sources (`memo:26`) says such figures "are labelled 'READ (search excerpt)' in the studies", but the memo itself does not flag them. Section 3, security (`memo:75`): the 16-attack-class paper, Check Point's escape and CaMeL are all "READ (search summary)" in `04:184-187`. Section 3, table (`memo:69`): AIMultiple's +7% latency is a search summary (`04:153`), unmarked. Section 5 (`memo:101`, `memo:103`): "Cursor writes MCP tool descriptions to a folder per server" and "Cursor writes long outputs, terminals and chat history to files" are "READ, through search summaries; cursor.com unreachable" (`05:84`). Section 6, first paragraph (`memo:107`): RAG-MCP, LiveMCPBench, ToolRet, the vendor benchmarks, Tool2Vec, Re-Invoke, Toolshed, the smelly-descriptions paper and ToolHijacker are all "READ (search excerpt)" (`06:5`, `06:55-117`). Fix: add "(search excerpt)" after each of these claims, or open each paragraph with "From search excerpts:".

M2. One row is marked search excerpt when it was read on the page. Memo section 3, table (`memo:70`): "research benchmarks, figures from search excerpts | two 2026 papers". "The Devil Is in the Interface" is "READ (microsoft.com abstract)" from a page fetched in full (`04:25`, `04:158`); only "Is Bash All You Need?" is a search summary (`04:159`). Fix: "Devil: READ, Microsoft Research abstract; Is Bash All You Need?: search summary". The same correction applies to section 10's list "the two 2026 bash and CodeAct papers" (`memo:166`).

M3. The "Is Bash" figure names a baseline the study does not state and drops the second benchmark. Memo section 3, table (`memo:70`): "bash alone beat a fixed typed catalog by 22 to 25 points with 19 to 72% fewer tokens". Study 04 reports "bash alone +21.8 to +24.5 points on The Agent Company and +4.8 to +7.4 on APEX-Agents", across five interfaces, of which "PTC over a fixed typed catalog" is one arm and typed tools another, without saying which arm the gain is measured against (`04:159`). Fix: "bash alone scored 21.8 to 24.5 points higher on The Agent Company and 4.8 to 7.4 on APEX-Agents than the typed-tool interfaces, with 19 to 72% fewer tokens (search summary; the baseline arm is not stated in the excerpt)".

M4. An inference is stated as a READ fact. Memo section 2 (`memo:51`): "Codex rebuilds every call made from a script as an ordinary call into the same tool runtime, so approvals and the sandbox still apply". The first half is READ; the consequence is labelled INFERRED in `02:100` ("INFERRED: ... approvals, the sandbox policy and the execution policy that judge a direct call judge the nested one, because it re-enters the same door"). Fix: "..., so, by inference from the shared door, approvals and the sandbox still apply".

M5. The experiment is credited with an exclusivity it did not test. Memo section 5 (`memo:99`): "only mcp2cli refuses a bad integer or an out-of-enum value before the call goes out". The bad integer was tried on mcp2cli alone (`experiments/05-cli-results/output.txt`, cases list: only "mcp2cli echo bad integer"); the bad enum was tried on mcp2cli, refused, and on mcporter, sent (`output.txt:51-52`, `:100-103`); mcpc's enum was not tested and philschmid's is "not checked" by reading (`05:63`). Fix: "mcp2cli refused a bad integer and an out-of-enum value before the wire; mcporter sent the out-of-enum value; the bad integer was tried on mcp2cli only".

M6. "Only GitHub annotates" contradicts the census. Memo section 4 (`memo:95`): "Only GitHub annotates its tools". Study 08 F6: "Docker: 24 of 755 tools carry annotations, 20 of them `readOnlyHint: true`"; Alibaba none (`08:108`). The study's own synthesis repeats the slip (`08:212`). Fix: "Annotations are sparse outside GitHub: Alibaba has none and 24 of Docker's 755 tools carry any".

M7. The paraphrase gap is given one cause, stated as measured. Memo section 6 (`memo:116`): "paraphrases reach 0.474, because the lexical leg has no stemming ("label" misses "labels")". Study 08 reads the misses as "word-form and synonym gaps" ("bookmark" against `star_repository`, Alibaba's "change requests" against "merge requests") and labels the stemming cause INFERRED (`08:181`). Fix: "paraphrases reach 0.474; the misses are word-form gaps the lexical leg cannot close without stemming ("label" against "labels") and synonym gaps no lexical leg closes ("bookmark" against `star_repository`) (INFERRED from the misses)".

M8. The retrieval numbers lose their validity limits. Section 6 (`memo:109-118`) and item 1 of section 9 (`memo:144`) rest on hit@k, but the memo nowhere carries study 08's limits: "No model chose tools here. Hit@k measures whether the right manifest is in what vfs returns, not whether a model ... picks the right tool"; one researcher wrote all 65 queries and 298 examples; "a single query moves a 46-query hit@1 by 0.022 and a 13-query bucket by 0.077"; "not an estimate of production accuracy" (`08:217-218`). Study 06's experiment carries the same kind of limit: 26 queries "written by the researcher after reading the tool list, which ... makes absolute figures meaningless" (`06:153`). Fix: add after the section 6 table: "These figures compare manifest variants and methods on one fixed query set written by one researcher; no model chose tools, one query moves hit@1 by 0.022, and the levels are not production accuracy."

M9. The cookbook finding drops its most telling half. Memo section 3, table (`memo:65`): "cookbook pair 110,473 → 15,919 tokens is not a controlled comparison: the mock API's data changes per process". Study 02 also records that "The two runs disagree on the answer" (ENG001 travel $6,753.62 against $9,177.88), that the pair is "valid only if both cells ran in one kernel, and nothing records that they did", and labels that reading INFERRED (`02:77`). Fix: "the cookbook pair 110,473 → 15,919 tokens disagrees on its own answer ($6,753.62 against $9,177.88), and its mock data changes per process (EXECUTED, three seeds), so it is valid only if both cells ran in one kernel, which nothing records (INFERRED)".

M10. The authority claim drops the limit study 04 asks a memo to state. Section 9, item 10 (`memo:153`): "This is the strongest claim vfs can make against the field, and it should be pinned, not asserted." Study 04: "A memo should state it with its limit: a prompt-injected program with write rights can still write anything those rights allow", and the claim that per-request authority blunts the CE-MCP attack classes "is reasoning from design, not an executed attack" (`04:268`, `04:283`). Study 07 adds that on a fresh node "anyone, anonymous included" can call every tool with the host's or the server's authority until two postures are set (`07:77`, `07:212`). Fix: append "Its limit: a prompt-injected program with write rights can still write anything those rights allow, the defence against the CE-MCP attack classes is unmeasured, and the drafted default lets every caller, anonymous included, call every tool until the host sets two postures."

M11. "Almost no studied design" re-enters the rights check, but several do. Memo "The answer" (`memo:9`): "every call already re-enters the rights check, which almost no studied design does". Codex sends every nested call through the same tool runtime (`02:100`); OpenAI's SDK keeps the runner's guardrails, hooks and approvals on program-owned calls and refuses a disallowed caller (`02:94`); executor, FastMCP and goose judge each call as the caller (`03:174`). Section 2 of the memo itself names three of these (`memo:51`). Fix: "every call re-enters the rights check, as Codex, executor, FastMCP and goose also do and Cloudflare's SDK, Anthropic's programmatic calling and most open-source designs do not".

M12. The sixth move adds an output cap the field does not share. Memo section 2, item 6 (`memo:47`): "Code sees full results; the model sees only what the code prints, under a timeout and an output cap." Study 03's consensus is "bounded by a timeout" (`03:187`); an output cap is in three of nine projects, and executor "says it sets none" (`03:177`). Fix: "..., under a timeout; an output cap is less common (Cloudflare about 6,000 tokens, Codex 10,000, UTCP's MCP server 200,000 characters, goose 1 MB; executor sets none)".

M13. One vendor-benchmark range mixes two measures and drops a higher figure. Memo section 6 (`memo:107`): "vendor benchmarks put BM25 top-1 at 14 to 34%". The 33.7% is Stacklok's selection accuracy for Anthropic's BM25 variant with Claude choosing, whose retrieval was 47.85%; the 14% figures are top-1 retrieval (StackOne, MCPProxy); and a summary attributes 64% BM25 over 4,027 tools to Arcade (`06:67`). Fix: "vendor benchmarks put BM25 top-1 retrieval at 14% (StackOne, MCPProxy) and BM25 tool selection at 34% (Stacklok), with one summary giving 64% (Arcade); hybrids near 94%; all vendor-run, from search excerpts".

## Verdict

The memo's structure, its section 4 census and disclosure figures, and its section 7 map of the tree match the studies. But seven High findings need correcting before the memo is cited: an overstated convergence in the answer, a uniqueness claim that three studied designs contradict, one miscounted and one misattributed evidence row, a "no comparison exists" sentence that the memo's own table contradicts, a fifty-fold understatement of the `cli` description's cost, and one margin stated for the wrong corpus. A superseding memo, or a corrected one before publication, should apply H1 to H7 and M1 to M13.
