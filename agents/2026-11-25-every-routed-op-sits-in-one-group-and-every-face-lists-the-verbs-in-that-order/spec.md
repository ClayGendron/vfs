# Every routed op sits in exactly one group, and every table and face lists the verbs in that one order

A draft, written on 2026-11-25. It is not reviewed, and nothing is built. It was checked against `dev` at `27f3d24d`. It serves criterion 2 of roadmap story K9 (`context/standards/roadmap.md:212`, `:218`), gate b. It is converted from [the archived spec 181](../../context/specifications/archive/181-the-public-surface-regrouped-by-kernel-group/spec.md), sections 1, 2 and 7, called spec 181 below, and it rests on the kernel rulings' decision 4, roadmap row D5 (flat names, decided) and roadmap row D63 (`run` kept, a lean). It was read again on 2026-11-25 against the sibling specs of this conversion that link it, name it, or add a routed op, a verb argument, a set or a table of ops, a method of a face or a list of tools (157-3, 170-1, 170-2, 170-4, 170-5, 171-1, 171-2, 175-3, 177-1, 177-4, 179-1, 179-2, 179-3, 180-1, 181-2 and 181-4), each written, not reviewed and not built; units 167-6, 167-7, 175-1 and 178-2 were read at the sentence that adds their argument. Decisions 14 to 16 are what that reading added, and decisions 5, 7 and 11 were amended in place for it.

## The outcome wanted

A reader of `vfs.ops` finds every routed op in exactly one named group: `filesystem`, `query`, `history`, `rights`, `derived` or `execution`. The `Op` literal, the needs table `VERB_RUNGS`, the parameter table `PARAMS` and the four public faces (`AsyncVirtualFileSystem`, `VirtualFileSystem`, `AsyncSession`, `Session`) list the 25 verbs in the order the groups give, and `cli` comes after them on every face. A drift test in `tests/test_ops.py` fails by name when a verb joins `Op` without a group, sits in two groups, or is defined out of order on a table or a face. Later verbs register in that one table, each in the landing that adds it: `versions` (story K8, unit 180-1) joins `history` first, and `accept_body` (unit 175-3) joins it second, ahead of `restore`. A method that is not an op, such as `install_extensions`, `add_mcp` or `add_tool`, is in no group and is defined before the verbs of its face. No call changes, no name changes, and no row a caller gets back changes.

## Terms

- **Group.** What kind of fact an op touches, or that it is built from other ops. One value of `OpGroup`.
- **Dispatch class.** How the router routes and gates an op: `MUTATING_OPS`, `TWO_PATH_OPS`, `READ_OPS`, `EXEC_OPS`, `GRANT_OPS` (`src/vfs/ops.py:68-98`). It is a second axis, independent of the group.
- **Surface order.** The 25 ops in the order of the group table, group by group. `SURFACE_ORDER` in `vfs.ops`.
- **Face.** One of the four public classes whose methods are the verbs.

## The problem

The op vocabulary says how each verb is routed and nothing about what kind of verb it is. A reader of the surface sees reads, then mutations, then search, then grants, with `restore` among the mutations and `tree` among the reads, and three tables that disagree on order. Story K9 needs one place where a later verb registers before the beta freezes the surface: K8's `versions` is the first. Without an order test, a verb added at the bottom of `base.py` or out of place in `PARAMS` passes every test today. Nothing here runs per call, so the reference deployment's multipliers do not apply; the cost of the problem is to readers and to the freeze.

## Decisions

1. **`OpGroup` has six names: `filesystem`, `query`, `history`, `rights`, `derived`, `execution`.** The first five are K9 criterion 2's (`roadmap.md:218`), which was written while `run` was to be retired. D63 keeps `run`, and `run` fits none of the five: it changes no stored fact and reads none in the sense of the kernel groups, and it has no definition from other ops, which is what `derived` means. Its dispatch class is already its own (`EXEC_OPS`, `ops.py:89`), the face banner on `dev` already names it `execution` (`base.py:1665`), and the README already shows it as its own family, `Execute` (`README.md:73`). The noun `execution` follows that banner and `how-we-name.md`'s rule that one concept has one noun. This adds a sixth name to K9's five; choice 1 records it.
2. **The table is, in this order:**
   - `filesystem`: `stat`, `ls`, `read`, `write`, `mkdir`, `delete`, `move`, `copy`.
   - `query`: `glob`, `grep`, `search`, `traverse`, `mkedge`, `rmedge`.
   - `history`: `restore`, `destroy`.
   - `rights`: `grant`, `revoke`, `grants`, `set_posture`, `add_member`, `remove_member`.
   - `derived`: `tree`, `edit`.
   - `execution`: `run`.

   Reason: spec 181 section 1's table, with four corrections against `dev`. `glean` and `graph` are `search` and `traverse` (the verb-names spec). Spec 181's `posture` is `set_posture`, the code's name (`ops.py:61`). `sweep` is `destroy` (decision 9). `run` is placed (decision 1). Inside a group the order is spec 181's: a name is found before its body is read, a row is made before it is removed, and a grant comes before the list of grants.
3. **The group order is the kernel's four groups, then the facets.** `filesystem`, `query`, `history` and `rights` are the four kinds of stored fact a storage answers for. `derived` and `execution` come after: the derived verbs are built from kernel calls with a storage push-down (spec 181 section 1.5), and `run` hands the call to a tool. `execution` is last so that `run` and the `cli` front sit together at the end of each face, under one banner, as they do on `dev` (`base.py:1665-1697`). The order follows AGENTS.md's rule that groups are ordered by the flow a caller follows.
4. **`vfs.ops` gains `OpGroup`, `OP_GROUPS` and `SURFACE_ORDER`, and nothing else.** `OpGroup` is a `Literal` of the six names, in the order of decision 3. `OP_GROUPS: Final[dict[OpGroup, tuple[Op, ...]]]` is the table of decision 2. `SURFACE_ORDER: Final[tuple[Op, ...]]` is `tuple(op for ops in OP_GROUPS.values() for op in ops)`. They sit directly under `Op`, since a derived constant sits under the public name it comes from (AGENTS.md, File organization). `GROUP_OF`, `CONTROL_METHODS` and a second name for `MANAGEMENT_METHODS`, which spec 181 section 2.1 adds, are left out: nothing in `src/` reads a group by op, and `MANAGEMENT_METHODS` is already in `ops.py` (`:110`). `ty` refuses a name in `OP_GROUPS` that is not an `Op` (EXECUTED on 2026-11-25: a scratch copy of the table naming `destroy` against today's `Op` gave one `invalid-assignment` diagnostic), so the type checker holds the members and the tests hold the coverage and the order.
5. **The `Op` literal, `VERB_RUNGS` and `PARAMS` are rewritten in the surface order.** Each entry moves and none changes. An entry moves whole, as it stands at build time: an argument a sibling has added to a verb's row of `PARAMS` rides that row, and its place among the row's arguments is that sibling's and follows the method's signature (`request_key`, "immediately before the authority row", unit 177-4's decision 3; `replace`, unit 167-7; `range`, unit 178-2; `verify`, unit 175-1; `case_mode` on `glob`, unit 167-6). The surface order is an order of verbs and says nothing of the arguments inside one. A `Literal` cannot be derived from a table, so `Op` is spelled out and a test pins it to `SURFACE_ORDER`. `ALL_OPS` and the dispatch classes are unchanged. The `ops.py` module docstring gains one paragraph on the two axes: the group says what kind of fact an op touches; the dispatch class says how it is routed and gated; `mkedge` is a mutation in `query`, `restore` a mutation in `history`, and `tree` a read in `derived`. It also says that `cli` is in no group. Reason: spec 181 section 2.1; the docstring states the axis that a reader would otherwise infer from the banners.
6. **`cli` is in no group, and it follows `run` on every face.** It stays in `FRONT_METHODS`, apart from `ALL_OPS` and `MANAGEMENT_METHODS`. Reason: the ruling of 2026-11-22 (Builds on). `cli` spans every mount and no storage answers it, while each verb inside a line is gated as its own op. This departs from K9 criterion 2's "`cli` → `derived`", as the roadmap's state line for K9 already records.
7. **Each face defines its verbs in the surface order, then `cli`, under one banner per group.** The methods move and their bodies do not change. On `base.py` the banners are `public methods — filesystem core`, `— query`, `— history`, `— rights`, `— derived` and `— execution`, in the file's 67-dash form; `cli` sits under `execution` after `run`. On `sync.py`, `session.py` and `sync_session.py` they are `filesystem core`, `query`, `history`, `rights`, `derived` and `execution`, in each file's own banner form. The members before the verbs (`sync.py`'s lifecycle, each face's mount administration and each session's `authority`, `closed` and `close`) stay where they are, and a public member a later unit adds that is not an op joins them there (decision 16). Reason: spec 181 section 7.2, adapted to four faces and to the `execution` group.
8. **The drift tests read definition order from each class's `__dict__`, never from comments.** A class's `__dict__` keeps the order of the definitions in its file, so the face test filters it to `ALL_OPS | FRONT_METHODS`. The banners are comments, and no test reads them: a test of comment text breaks on a reworded banner and proves nothing about the surface.
9. **The history group names `destroy`; if this unit is built before the deletes spec lands, the slot holds `sweep`.** Built after the deletes spec, the table is written with `destroy`. Built before it, the table holds `sweep` in the same place, and the deletes spec's rename then carries `OP_GROUPS` too. Its landing cannot miss the table: `test_every_op_sits_in_exactly_one_group` fails as soon as `Op` names `destroy` and the table does not. Reason: the reassessment asks for `destroy` "if it lands first"; neither spec waits on the other, and the test makes the second landing correct.
10. **`OpGroup`, `OP_GROUPS` and `SURFACE_ORDER` stay in `vfs.ops` and do not join `vfs.__all__`.** Reason: decision 4 of the kernel rulings says a group's name is not a public name and freezes nothing. Kept out of `__all__`, the names reach no generated reference page and no freeze list, so a later rename of a group costs one module.
11. **A later op registers in `Op`, in one group of `OP_GROUPS` and in the tests' one literal table, and a later method that is not an op registers nowhere here.** `tests/test_ops.py` holds the table once, as a module constant `REGISTERED_GROUPS`. `test_the_groups_are_the_registered_table` compares `OP_GROUPS` with it. `test_expected_vocabulary` (`tests/test_ops.py:74`), which on `dev` holds the vocabulary as a second literal, a set of 25 names, is rewritten to compare `ALL_OPS` with the names of that table, so a story that adds a verb edits one literal and no second list. K9 criterion 2 asks for exactly that: "its drift test is the one place later verbs register". Without the rewrite a landing that follows a sibling's Tests section, which names `test_the_groups_are_the_registered_table` alone (unit 180-1), fails on the second literal. Choice 7 records it. K8's `versions` joins `history`, and decision 14 gives its place and `accept_body`'s. The kernel methods later stories add (`fs.calls`, `answer`, `resume`, `cancel`; `roadmap.md:311`, `:348`, `:366`) are management methods, not ops, so they join `MANAGEMENT_METHODS` and no group; decision 16 places them and the methods the siblings add on the faces.
12. **`base.py`'s size entry moves to the exact count the file ends at.** The regroup adds one banner to the file's public verbs (six where there were five): four lines, three for the banner and one blank line. Nothing else in the file grows. Raising the entry by that amount in the same landing has precedent: `885b6a29` (3,091 to 3,103) and `f36595fe` (3,092 to 3,098) each raised it with the change that grew it. Moving code out of `base.py` to make room would mix a refactor into a reorder whose diff is meant to be moves and banners alone.
13. **The README's verb table is not changed.** It keeps its families, Read, Find, Mutate and Execute (`README.md:68-73`). The groups are named in `ops.py` and nowhere in the published docs. Reason: decision 4 of the kernel rulings leaves this to the conversion, the `Find` label is a landed ruling (the verb-names spec), and a README table relabelled by groups would need its own parsing test for a list that freezes nothing. Choice 2 records it.
14. **Inside `history` the order is `versions`, `accept_body`, `restore`, `destroy`, and each of the two later verbs is written into the table by the landing that adds it.** This unit's landing names neither: both are absent from `Op` on `dev`, and `ty` refuses a name in `OP_GROUPS` that is not an `Op` (decision 4). A later op takes its place by decision 2's reasons and by one more rule: the verbs one storage family answers stay next to each other, because unit 179-2's drift test reads the families in order and expects `SURFACE_ORDER` ("Inside a group the families follow 181-1's surface order", its decision 2).
    - `versions` is first. Unit 180-1's decision 9 puts it "first in the `history` group, before restore", and the table agrees: it reads the kept versions and changes none, and the verbs that only read lead `filesystem` (`stat`, `ls`, `read`) and `query` (`glob`, `grep`, `search`, `traverse`).
    - `accept_body` is second. Unit 175-3's decision 3 adds it to `Op`, `MUTATING_OPS`, `DEVELOPER_OPS`, `VERB_RUNGS` and `PARAMS` and to the four faces, and asks for `history` "beside `restore`". The group is confirmed: the verb writes no byte of a body, and what it changes is the record of a version, a new version number, the recorded hash and one displaced snapshot in the version rows. Its place is before `restore` and not after it, because `restore` and `destroy` are one family, `SupportsTrash` (unit 179-2's decision 2), and `accept_body` is a family of its own, `SupportsAcceptBody` (unit 175-3's decision 3). EXECUTED on 2026-11-25, a prototype of the family rule over the 27 names, with the two families the two units add beside `SupportsTrash`: the orders `versions, accept_body, restore, destroy` and `versions, restore, destroy, accept_body` read back as the surface order family by family, and `versions, restore, accept_body, destroy` does not. Of the two that hold, the first is the one beside `restore`, and it keeps the verb that removes for good last, as decision 2 has a row made before it is removed. Choice 3 records it.
    - Each landing edits `Op`, `OP_GROUPS`, the tables of decision 5, the four faces and `REGISTERED_GROUPS`, and the tests of this spec then hold it: a verb left out of the table fails `test_every_op_sits_in_exactly_one_group`, and one defined out of place fails its table's or its face's case by name. With both landed the vocabulary holds 27 ops. A dispatch class is the sibling's to choose and is the second axis: `versions` is in `READ_OPS`, and `accept_body` is in `MUTATING_OPS` and `DEVELOPER_OPS`.
15. **The surface order binds the vocabulary tables of `vfs.ops` and `vfs.params` that hold a row for every op, and the four faces; a set of ops, a table keyed by part of the vocabulary, a lookup built from the sets and a list on the wire take no order from it.**
    - *A table of `vfs.ops` or `vfs.params` with one row for every op is written in the surface order.* On `dev` those are `VERB_RUNGS` and `PARAMS`, with the `Op` literal (EXECUTED, What the code does today). `test_the_order_test_names_every_table_keyed_by_the_whole_vocabulary` scans the module-level dicts of `vfs.ops` and `vfs.params` and fails by name when one whose keys are `ALL_OPS` is not a case of `test_the_vocabulary_tables_list_the_surface_order`, so a total table a later unit adds is held without a rule that someone must remember. Choice 6 records it.
    - *A table keyed by part of the vocabulary keeps its own order.* `RULES` and `VALUE_RULES` on `dev`, the replay table `REPLAY` of unit 177-1 (fifteen write-gated verbs, its decision 1) and the table `METHOD_ATOMICITY` of unit 179-3 (the write-gated verbs with `rebuild` and `refresh`, in `protocol.py`) are looked up by op, and each is pinned to its own set by its own unit. `_FAMILY_OPS` is unit 179-2's, and that unit's own test holds it to `SURFACE_ORDER`.
    - *A lookup built from the sets has no written order.* The default projections (`src/vfs/results/projection.py:49-61`) hold a row for each of the 25 ops (EXECUTED on 2026-11-25: `KNOWN_FUNCTIONS == ALL_OPS`), but nine are written out and sixteen are filled by a loop over `MUTATING_OPS | GRANT_OPS`, two frozensets, and the keys are plain strings, with a fallback for a name the table does not hold. No reader lists it, and a verb a sibling adds to it (`versions`, unit 180-1's decision 9) is one more row wherever that unit writes it.
    - *A set of ops is a class on the second axis, not a group.* `AGENT_OPS` and `DESTRUCTIVE_OPS` (unit 171-1's decisions 2 and 3), `KEYED_OPS` (unit 177-4's decision 1) and `IDEMPOTENT_OPS` (unit 177-1) are frozensets in `vfs.ops`, as the dispatch classes are. None is a group, no group is derived from one, and a frozenset has no order to pin. The two-axes paragraph of decision 5 covers them with its sentence on dispatch classes and names none of them, since none exists when this unit is built.
    - *A list of tools on the wire is not a face.* Unit 171-2's decision 6 is "Return names in lexical order", for the set `(AGENT_OPS & fs.capabilities()) | {"cli"}` cut by the host's allowlist, and that order stands. Reason: a group's name is documentation and freezes nothing (the kernel rulings' decision 4; decision 10 here), and a tool list in group order would publish the order of the groups to every client, so that a later regroup, which today costs one module, would change what `tools/list` returns. A lexical list depends on the names alone, and the names are what the freeze holds. It is also the same for every subset the capabilities and the allowlist leave.
