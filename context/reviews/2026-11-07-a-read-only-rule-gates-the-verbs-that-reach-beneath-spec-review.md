## The author's questions, answered first

1. **Is a static check over the map's overrides (decision 3), with no storage read, the right shape, including for a file-shaped target?** EXECUTED and INFERRED. The shape is right in what it ignores and wrong in one thing it ignores. Ignoring storage is right: a copy or move onto an absent `/proj2` is exactly the case a row probe would miss (EXECUTED: today it lands rows under `/proj2/locked`), and a probe would add a read per gated path and a race between the probe and the write. A file-shaped target is refused only when the operator declared a rule under a file's path; that refusal is conservative and harmless, since no live row can sit beneath a file (INFERRED). What the static check must not ignore is the call itself: `delete(..., cascade=False)` is refused by storage on a non-empty directory, so its effect never reaches beneath, yet decision 2 gates it as a subtree verb, and the router's own `remove_mount` uses exactly that call (H1). And the static check reads the address as a subtree root even where the address is not one: the trash view, whose retention sweep reaches every trashed row of the mount (M2).
2. **Are the five ops in decision 2 exactly the verbs whose effect reaches beneath their path, and does `_gate_entry` see every route they take?** READ and EXECUTED. Of the ten `MUTATING_OPS` (`src/vfs/ops.py:64-66`), the five are the ones that act on a subtree: delete, restore and sweep are `_BUSY_GUARDED_OPS` (`base.py:122`), and move (source and destination) and copy (destination) are busy-guarded with `subtree=True` at `base.py:2526-2529`. `write`, `edit`, `mkdir`, `mkedge` and `rmedge` address one row; the grant writes (`grant`, `revoke`, `posture`, `add_member`, `remove_member`) write rights rows, and the map still refuses any write those rights would admit under a read rule, so they need not join (L2). Every route the five take reaches `_gate_entry` with the op: the single route at `base.py:1748` (delete, restore, sweep), the grouped route at `base.py:1795` (delete and restore by observation), and the paired route at `base.py:2537` (move and copy). Because the busy guard refuses any subtree verb whose region holds a bind site (`base.py:2930-2942`), a subtree verb never spans mounts, so the terminal's `_permission_layers` are every map that can hold a rule beneath it. Two exceptions to "exactly": `delete` with `cascade=False` does not reach beneath (H1), and `sweep` at the trash view reaches more than beneath (M2). Two callers the spec does not name also take these routes: `add_mount`'s restore of a tombstoned site (`base.py:547`) and `remove_mount`'s strict rmdir (`base.py:575`) (H1).
3. **Are the acceptance criteria buildable as written against the current test files?** EXECUTED. Criteria 1, 2, 5 and 6 are. Criterion 6 holds today under an emulation of the Design: the four named files pass (331) and so does the whole suite (4,580). Criterion 3 is not: its seed writes "as the system actor" are refused by the map, because the gate is actor-blind (`base.py:2904` passes no authority), and its "`restore('/proj')` after a system delete" needs a delete of `/proj` that this spec itself refuses for every actor. Criterion 4's successes also need rows to exist first (a delete of an absent path is `not_found`, not a success), and it names no seeding (M1).
