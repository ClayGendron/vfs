## Scale and the production posture

- Planning: one rewrite a root, read under the walk's deadline every 64 steps. Whole-side and ancestor compositions are bounded by two an entry and the bind depth, times the arms (READ).
- The composed prefilter: at most 200 patterns a side, composed and compiled on the loop. Compiling the same patterns is today's storage-side cost for a small call. Only the composing moves, from the router's worker to storage, and it is cheap (INFERRED). Per verb call, at about 20 calls a second at peak, this is the same order as today's small calls.
- Statements: unchanged builders, chunked by `arm_budget` and the root fan's budgets, under Oracle's `IN` cap and SQL Server's binds. Nothing grows with batch size (READ).
- The gate: R times P judged paths, the same product as today. Only anonymous callers pay it, off the loop, chunked under the wall, with the per-root direction named. That is a suboptimal profile named honestly, not a cap. The saving is the composing and re-parsing (EXECUTED by the author, 1.66 s against 0.13 s at 640,000 paths; I did not re-time it).
