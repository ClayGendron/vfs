## Findings, most severe first

1. **H1. A delta re-posts chunk ids the base still holds, and the scorer counts them twice** ([Decisions](../specifications/active/2026-10-31-refresh-the-incremental-index-verb.md#decisions), 6 and 12; [Acceptance criteria](../specifications/active/2026-10-31-refresh-the-incremental-index-verb.md#acceptance-criteria), 4 and 17). Decision 12 rests on "a chunk id appears in one segment of the live set", and decision 6 on "a ghost's id can never resolve to a live chunk". Both are false whenever the chunk pass keeps an entry's chunk rows. That happens on two ordinary paths. The fingerprint-skip law keeps the rows of a same-body overwrite (`indexing.py:261` to 265), and a trash restore never re-splits: delete demotes `encoded` and leaves `chunked` (`trash.py:446` to 452). EXECUTED with `chunk_id_probe.py` after a full `reindex()`: a changed body gets new ids, as the spec expects; a same-body overwrite and a trash then restore each end the chunk pass `chunked ∧ indexable ∧ ~encoded ∧ live`, in the delta's predicate, with the same chunk ids the base's `lex_docs` already posts. The publish deletes the base's `lex_docs` rows for those ids, but not its `lex_postings`, so the id sits in the blocks of both segments and resolves through the delta's row. The scorer sums per chunk id over every block (`lexical.rs:864`). EXECUTED with `double_post_probe.py`: the same chunk posted in a base slot and a delta slot under the base's idf scores 1.3299 where it scored 0.6650 alone, a ratio of 2.0. Consequence if built: every same-body rewrite and every restore outranks its peers by about twice its score until the next full rebuild, on every engine and in both the unscoped and the visible path. At the reference deployment's nightly batch of 1,000,000 files, re-ingest of unchanged bodies is the common case. Criterion 4 tests a re-split, and criterion 17's oracle scores "the same blocks", so neither can see it. Fix text, a sentence in decision 12: "The chunk pass keeps an entry's chunk rows when its body is unchanged (the fingerprint-skip law) and a trash restore keeps them too, so the lexical delta posts only the covered chunks whose id has no `lex_docs` row in the live set, read by `epoch IN (S) AND chunk_id IN (keys)` on the `(epoch, chunk_id)` key in membership chunks; the publish deletes `lex_docs` rows only for the covered entries' chunk ids the chunk table no longer holds; an entry whose every chunk id the live set already resolves posts no lexical row and is flipped `encoded` with the rest. Then a chunk id is in the lexical rows of exactly one live segment." Decision 6's last two sentences take the same qualification. New criterion: "A same-body overwrite and a trash then restore of an indexed entry, followed by `refresh()`: no chunk id has `lex_postings` blocks in two live segments, and search scores the entry's chunk exactly as it scored it before the write. Test: `tests/storage/database/test_search.py::TestLiveSet::test_a_kept_chunk_id_is_posted_once`."
2. **H2. The visible lexicon under a live set is not decided** ([Decisions](../specifications/active/2026-10-31-refresh-the-incremental-index-verb.md#decisions), 28; [Design](../specifications/active/2026-10-31-refresh-the-incremental-index-verb.md#design), the readers paragraph). A partial caller's search runs `_visible_lexicon` (`search.py:834` to 889), the landed visible-set law of [the grants spec](../specifications/archive/058-row-level-permission-grants/spec.md) (its decision 7), and the spec names three of its helpers but not the function. That function takes one `SummaryRow` per term from `stats.terms[term]` and uses it three ways: the union cap, `sum(stats.terms[term].df ...)` (`search.py:868`), which `candidate_ids` treats as a hard limit on survivors (`postings.rs:278`); the bound maxima, `decode_summary(row.blocks).max_weights` indexed by each block's `block_no` (`search.py:881`, `:886`); and the rescale `term_idf / row.idf` (`search.py:880`). Decision 28 makes `terms` hold the first slot's row, so as written every later slot's blocks would be bounded by the first slot's summary and idf, and the union capped by the first slot's df. Decision 28's export rule also carries no scope, though the grants law requires a partial caller's export to be visible-set. READ, the unscoped path: `view` is `None` only when read rights are whole (`backend.py:1702` to 1703). Consequence if built: under-bounded blocks prune true hits from a partial caller's top-k, an index past the first summary's last block raises, the union drops visible chunks, and a builder who applies decision 28's sums to the export shows a partial caller corpus-wide counts. Criterion 16 would pass in each case. Fix text, a paragraph in decision 28: "A caller whose read rights are not whole is served by `_visible_lexicon`, as the grants spec's decision 7 lays down, over the live set: `N` and the mean length from `_visible_corpus` over `epoch IN (S)`, each term's visible `df` from the union of its blocks across every slot met with the visible chunks, one visible idf per term; each block's bound is rescaled from its own slot's summary and stored idf, `max_weight_slot * idf_v / idf_slot * max(1, avg_dl_v / avg_dl_base)`; the union's cap is the sum of `df` over the term's slots; and that caller's `lexical_stats` export is the visible lexicon's, never the corpus-wide sums above, which only a caller with whole read rights receives." New criterion: "Under a base and two deltas, a partial caller's search equals the oracle's BM25 over the visible chunks under visible statistics, the bound of every fetched block is at least its true maximum under them, and its export's `n_docs` and each `df` are the visible counts. Test: `tests/storage/database/test_search.py::TestLiveSet::test_a_partial_caller_scores_and_exports_the_visible_live_set`."
