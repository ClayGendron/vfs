## The author's questions, answered first

1. **Is a member limit, a declared-total limit and a ratio limit consistent with "never design toward a hard scale cap", with how `RenderLimits` is used, and honest and recoverable for a legitimate large file.** Consistent in kind: they are per-render processing limits a host may raise, like `chars` and `pages`, which `seam.py:77-84` already lets a host raise (READ), and none limits what vfs stores: a refused body is still written, versioned and readable as bytes (READ, the read path in `reads.py:875-910` at `5d47a21a` returns the row with `content` null). The member default is justified by measurement (EXECUTED: a 1,000-slide deck with notes has 4,039 members, about four a slide, as the spec says). The 1 GiB default is honest about docx and pptx, whose libraries hold what they read, but not about xlsx, whose worksheets openpyxl streams (finding M2). What a user sees is honest: the caller gets `render_status` `failed` and a warning "no text rendering (failed)", the operator gets a `render_detail` naming the limit and the excess (READ; `render_detail` reaches no caller, EXECUTED by `git grep`, only `topology.py` and `writes.py` copy it). It is not recoverable for entries already refused: raising the limit retries nothing until the bytes or the generation change, which decision 13 says and decision 10 contradicts with "loses nothing" (finding M2).
2. **Only stored and deflate, and unique part names, inferred.** Not settled from the standard: no local file carries the Open Packaging Conventions text; the libraries name ISO/IEC 29500 only in the docstrings of their mapping modules (`docx/opc/spec.py:1`, `pptx/opc/spec.py:1`, READ). Settled from the writers: all three write only deflate (`docx/opc/phys_pkg.py:109`, `pptx/opc/serialized.py:242`, `openpyxl/writer/excel.py:291`, READ). The cost of a wrong method inference is small and bounded: a method zipfile does not implement (Deflate64, method 9, among them) already lands `corrupt` today, so the only files that newly stop rendering are Office files whose members were rewritten in bzip2, LZMA or (on 3.14) zstd by a general archiver; those render `ok` today (EXECUTED) and would land `failed`, and no limit a host can raise brings them back, because `OOXML_METHODS` is a constant (INFERRED). The cost of a wrong uniqueness inference is the same shape: a file whose directory repeats an exact name, which the libraries open today (the spec's EXECUTED line), would land `failed`. The exact-string test is the right one for the bound even though the conventions compare part names without regard to ASCII case (INFERRED from the standard as I know it): zipfile resolves names exactly, so case variants are distinct members whose declared sizes the total already counts.
3. **The guarantee and its residual.** Decision 11's own sentence is exactly as strong as the code makes it: a read returns at most the declared size, because the reader uses the directory's sizes and method, never the local header's (EXECUTED: a member whose directory size was lowered to 10 returned at most 10 bytes and then failed its CRC). The residual is named, has a direction, and holds no open question. But it is larger in practice than its wording suggests, and the spec's opening, decision 8 and the Intent claim more than decision 11 grants (finding M1).
4. **`corrupt` and `failed`.** The index treats both alike: `RenderCounts.record` counts both under `failed` (`render.py:116-129` at `5d47a21a`, READ), the read warning is the same unsupported, non-retryable record with the status in its data (`reads.py:875-910`, READ), and the skip law retries neither (the binary-bodies spec, section 6, READ). So the choice is a word on the row, not a behaviour. Limits as `failed` match the Builder's ratio guard (`markdown.py:232-235`, READ). The method and duplicate refusals are not limits; they are a container the format does not admit, which is what the seam calls `corrupt` (finding L2). No revision bump is needed: the generation is the renderer's name, its `revision` and its library's version (`seam.py:282-285`, READ), none of which changes, so nothing is re-dirtied and nothing re-renders at landing (INFERRED from that code).
5. **Cost at the reference deployment.** The multiplier is one render, not one index visit: the render stage selects only rows whose stamp misses (the binary-bodies spec, section 5, READ), so the check runs once per Office body written and once per generation change. Measured on the largest ordinary containers I built (EXECUTED): a 1,000-slide deck with notes, 1,886,675 bytes and 4,039 members, read 320,683 bytes and held a 2,720 KiB peak, 690 bytes a member; a 50,000-row, 12-column sheet, 1,998,393 bytes and 9 members, read 616 bytes and held 9 KiB; the docx fixture read 1,163 bytes. ## Premortem

The spec was built as written and failed. First, the next security audit reopens the decompression finding: a container of a few hundred kilobytes that understates one member passes every limit and makes python-docx hold hundreds of MiB, and a pptx can repeat that once per member at open; that is M1. ## Findings, most severe first

The named direction says closing this needs the libraries to accept an archive vfs opens for them; it does not: a verifying pass inside the check, reading each deflated member in 64 KiB chunks against its declared size plus one byte and refusing any member whose stream runs past its declaration or fails its CRC, refused both hostile containers with a 0.3 MiB peak, and on an ordinary 8,084,987-byte-declared docx it found nothing, held 0.4 MiB and cost 0.002 s against 0.018 s for python-docx's own open (EXECUTED, wall time in a probe, never in a test).
