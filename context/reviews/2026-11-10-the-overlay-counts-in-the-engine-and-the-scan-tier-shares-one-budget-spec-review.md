## The author's questions, answered first

1. **Is dropping a purged overlay entry from the answer silently, as grep's verify tier does, the right rule, or should the call carry a record saying an entry vanished between the two reads?** READ `grep.py:365`, `glean.py:465-472` and `:380-391`, the constitution's Article 2, and INFERRED. Silent drop is the right rule, with one sentence added. Three reasons. First, the entry does not exist at the moment the answer is cut: a `read` of its path would return `not_found`, so an answer without it is the true answer at the preview read's point, and a retry is idempotent and complete. Second, grep already drops a body that did not come back with no record (`grep.py:365`), so a record here would make the two verbs disagree about the same event in the same tier, the gap this spec exists to close. Third, the closed taxonomy has no honest kind for it: `truncated` says a budget cut the answer, which is false, and `not_found` on a path the caller never named would be a record about another principal's lawful purge, noise an agent cannot act on. What a record would buy is one thing, and the spec should say it instead: the `[:limit]` slice at `glean.py:465` runs before the drop, so an answer can hold fewer than `limit` rows while candidates sit below the cut, and glean emits no record when the limit itself cuts (the only limit-shaped records, `glean.py:388-391`, fire when the index window is full and short), so an agent at `limit=10` that gets 9 cannot tell a short corpus from this race; the window is a few milliseconds, on two engines, for an overlay entry in the top `limit`, and the next call answers in full, so one disclosing sentence is proportionate and a top-up fetch of the next ordered entries is not (L2 carries the sentence). The alternative, re-slicing after the drop, would need a second preview fetch over the next candidates in a loop, work for a race the spec can name in one line.
2. **Does decision 7's second read on SQL Server and Oracle (no `op_isolation` pin) break Article 5 "Reads are snapshots" in a way the disclosure does not cover, and if so what should the spec say?** READ `dialects.py:508`, `:576`, `:664-672`, `engine.py:1044-1052`, `grep.py:362-366`, `git show 682182f:...glean.py:984-994`, Article 5, and INFERRED. It does not break Article 5 in a new kind, and the disclosure covers the two effects but not the article or the fact that the departure is engine-level and older than this spec; the spec should say both. The fact: on SQLite every op connection opens `BEGIN` (`engine.py:1049`) and Postgres and MariaDB stamp `REPEATABLE READ` on the op connection (`dialects.py:508`, `:576`, `:671-672`), so on three of five engines one glean call is one snapshot and the preview read sees the counted body exactly; on SQL Server and Oracle no isolation is declared, every statement reads at its own point, and that was already true of the nominee fetch, the surrogate fetch and the chunked body fetches of the overlay at `682182f` (`content_for_entries` chunks by `membership_budget`, so a 1,100-row Oracle overlay was already two body statements at two points), and of grep, which pairs a nominee row from one statement with a body from a later one (`grep.py:362-366`). What this spec changes on those two engines is the effect: before, an overlay observation's fields came from one point and its body from another; after, its score comes from one body and its preview from a later one, so a preview can lack the term that scored it, and a purged body makes the entry absent. Decision 7 discloses both effects and names neither the article nor the pre-existing shape, and the constitution's preamble asks a deviation to name the principle, the deviation and the expected expiry; Article 5's bullets carry no RFC keyword, so this is a write-in and not a High, but the honest sentence is cheap: "this is the statement-level consistency every multi-statement read in both verbs already has on the two engines that pin no `op_isolation`, the one departure from Article 5's one coherent point-in-time view that this spec touches; it adds a point in time and no new kind, and it expires when those two profiles declare a snapshot isolation for op connections, which is an experiment on its lock cost" (L3 carries it). Nothing else is needed: retries are idempotent, no handle is held open, and the `StaleSnapshot` redrive is about the index pointer and rightly untouched.
