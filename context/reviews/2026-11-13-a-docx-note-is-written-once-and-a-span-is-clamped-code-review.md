The other Builder callers, EXECUTED with `probe5.py` (the build's own `pptx.py`, `xlsx.py` and `pdf.py`, unchanged since `18b76739`, on the base Builder and on the build's): a pptx deck with a table, a two-sheet workbook with pipes and a far cell, and a three-page PDF render identically at every budget from 1 to 80 past their length and at the default. The xlsx spec's Design states what it stands on (READ, its "What this spec stands on from the docx spec" paragraph): `remaining` zero once stopped and never negative, `kept_length` the characters neither whitespace nor NUL, and `table(rows, *, width=None)` drawing each row after the line before, the clock before every row after the first, a short row padded, and no width reading every row first; the build leaves exactly that. Its decision 6 assumes a stopped row costs "the remaining characters plus one cell" between two clock reads, which is what the pull-before-clock form of `table` gives: the one row a passed clock lets through is cut at `remaining` by the renderer's own stop. Decision 13's cost, READ in `storage/backends/database/render.py` and the refresh spec: a new generation makes every docx row stale for `select_render_dirty`, today's `reindex()` re-renders it, and once the refresh spec lands its render step reads `pending` rows only, so the next `rebuild()` pays, as the third departure says. The bump is needed although no ordinary document's text changes (the corpus): without it, every entry stamped `failed` today by an orphan continuation, a quoted id or an unrecognised style value would keep its failure under the skip law, and every entry that stores a repeated note's copies would keep them. At the reference deployment, at a tenth of 10,000,000 files, that is about 1,000,000 renders and their roughly 10,000,000 chunks re-split and re-posted once, at the per-index-visit multiplier, in one pass; nothing recurs. Not reached: python-docx versions after 1.2.0, which do not exist on PyPI as I write; documents written by Word itself, since I generated every document with python-docx.

### Scale

Method: `growth.py` measured each bound on the base and the build at a scale k and at 4k, counting text reads, walks up a column, style elements yielded, inline walks, characters handed to the Builder, cells drawn and note characters read; the ratios are in the criteria above.
