## The author's questions, answered first

1. **Decision 17 has a refresh with no published base refuse and name reindex, rather than run the full build. Is that the right contract?** READ and INFERRED. Yes. Decision 1 makes the two verbs distinguishable by cost (the constitution's Article 5, "cost is part of the contract"), and a refresh that quietly became the O(corpus) build would be the one case where the caller could not tell them apart; a loud refusal keeps the contract honest and costs the operator one scheduled `reindex` they must run anyway. The class fits today's usage: `backend.py:1211` already classifies "binaries are still pending a rendering; run reindex again" as `ErrorKind.unavailable`, so a non-retryable `unavailable` whose detail names `reindex` is the existing idiom, not a new one. The departure from [study 04](../research/studies/2026-10-31-incremental-index-refresh/04-design-options.md) decision 6 is named in the spec, as the standard requires. One thing the decision should also say, because the build would otherwise choose: a refresh with a published base and nothing dirty is not this case and must mint nothing (H2).
2. **Decision 6 keeps glean free of ghost chunk ids by deleting the covered entries' lex_docs rows across the live set at publish. Is that sufficient, and is it bounded on Oracle and SQL Server?** READ. Sufficient for the `KeyError`, by the shape of the code: `_entries_for_chunks` (`glean.py:565`) joins `lex_docs` to `entry` and keeps a chunk only when a row names it; a chunk with no owner is skipped at `owner.get(chunk_id) is None` (`glean.py:583`); `wanted` for `_chunk_texts` is built from `fused.chunks` of resolved hits only (`glean.py:395`), so `_observe`'s `sources.chunks[chunk_id]` (`glean.py:1036`) is only ever asked for a chunk that resolved. Two conditions carry it. First, every `lex_docs` read must carry the live set, not just `_entries_for_chunks`: `_chunk_ids_for` (`glean.py:454`), `_visible_corpus` (776 to 793) and `_visible_chunks` (834) are pinned to one epoch today and the Design does not name them (M1). Second, the delete must run over the live set as it stands before the `published_at` stamp, so the delta's own fresh `lex_docs` rows survive it; the spec's "across the live set" is right only in that order (L4). The `lex_postings` ghosts stay and are scored, so at high churn they eat ranking slots; the probe loop at `glean.py:313` to 333 deepens twice and then records "scope probe budget", which is the existing bounded behaviour, so this is a note. Bounded: the delete is `epoch IN (S) AND entry_id IN (chunk)`, served by `ix_vfs_lex_docs_entry (epoch, entry_id)` (`rows.py:734`), chunked by `membership_budget - |S|` under decision 9; on Oracle (`in_list_budget` 1,000, `dialects.py:532`) a 10,000-entry dirty set with 49 segments is eleven statements of at most 951 ids each. Bounded in the entry dimension; the segment dimension is H3.
3. **Decision 15 relies on the existing reclaim rule to sweep every delta after a full publish. Does that hold under every interleaving, including a delta minted while a full build is in progress?** READ and INFERRED. The sweep holds whenever the full's number exceeds the delta's, and the mint gives that in every case but one: `build_epoch` mints `max(built, pointer) + 1` (`indexing.py:405` to 406) where `built` is `max(gram_epochs.epoch)`, and the `gram_epochs` row is inserted at the end of the build transaction (`indexing.py:447` to 450). A delta minted while a full is mid-build takes the same number, collides on the posting PK (the full re-posts every gram the delta has), and the loser redrives; a redriving delta mints the same number again until the full's row lands, so it exits as `conflict`, which the study's own table says. A delta built after the full's build committed and before the full published mints above the full (`max(built)` now includes the full) with `base_epoch` the old pointer; the full publishes, the reclaim deletes `epoch < current` and leaves that delta standing above the pointer with a base nobody points at. That orphan is swept one rebuild late, which alone is tidy-up, but its flips are not: the delta flipped `encoded` on `(entry, version)` pairs the full never scanned, so after the full publishes those entries are `encoded` with their only postings in a segment outside the live set, invisible to the index tier and excluded from the scan tier until the next full rebuild. The spec excludes this by decision 20's "while a full rebuild runs, no refresh runs", which is a property of the lease; `claim_reindex_lease` takes a lease whose heartbeat is five minutes stale (`indexing.py:162`), the module docstring calls the lease an arbiter and not a guard, and `_gram_phases` checks `lost` only at phase boundaries (`backend.py:1392`, `1400`), so a paused runner that resumes publishes. Decision 14's "needs no generation precondition" is where the gap sits; one predicate closes it (H1).
4. **Decision 12 concatenates lexical blocks across segments and treats each (term, segment) summary as its own term for select_blocks. Does the scorer keep top-k correct under that?** READ. Yes, and the bound only loosens. `score` (`lexical.rs:625` to 690) sums per chunk id over every block whose slot names an idf; a chunk id lives in one segment, so a chunk gathers at most one slot per query term and its sum is the true BM25 sum under the slot's idf. Pruning uses `rest`, the summed bounds of the slots not yet visited (`lexical.rs:633` to 637); splitting a term into per-segment slots adds the other segments' maxima to `rest` where the truth is at most one of them, so `block.bound + remaining < theta` fires less often, never more. `select_blocks` (`lexical.rs:335` to 350) sums the later summaries' maxima into `rest` the same way, and `competing_blocks` (301 to 328) places a candidate into a block by `first_ids.partition_point`, so a candidate from another segment can only add a block to fetch, never drop one. `theta` is the k-th partial score, which is exact. Two conditions: delta block bounds must be computed under the statistics the reader scores with (decision 11 does this), and the per-slot idf the reader passes must be the stored one for that `(term, segment)` (the Design's `lexical_stats` over the set does this). What is missing is a criterion that pins the claim; the oracle in `tests/support/oracles/lexical.py` is the referee (M3).
5. **Do decision 13's generation CAS and decision 14's pointer CAS plus generation bump close every delta-vs-delta and delta-vs-full race, including a reader straddling them?** READ and INFERRED. Delta against delta: both read `(base, G)`; the first CAS moves `G` and the second fails `index_generation = :seen`; closed. Delta after a full: the full moved the pointer, the delta's `current_gram_epoch = :base` fails; closed. Reader straddling either: the reader's first read takes `G`, the live-set statement and every fetch follow, the publish bumps `G`, the second read differs and raises `StaleSnapshot`; the reclaim that follows a full publish is a separate transaction, as today (`backend.py:1408`), and a reader that saw rows vanish also sees `G` move; closed. Full after a delta on the same base, where the delta published between the full's scan and the full's publish: the full's CAS is on the pointer alone and succeeds, and the delta's flips survive with its postings outside the live set; open under a lease lapse (question 3, H1). The fix is the mirror of decision 13: the full's CAS carries `index_generation = :seen` read with the pointer at build start; in the normal flow no delta publishes during a full, so the predicate costs nothing.
6. **Is the spec one landing, or should it be cut, and where?** READ and INFERRED. It is one landing as written and may stay one; CLAUDE.md says green does not mean small. If the author wants a cut, there is exactly one safe place: the Design's first stage (the four columns, the format bump, `index_generation` returned by `pointer_with_overlay`, both readers comparing the generation, and decision 21's demotion) is behaviour-preserving and green on its own, with criteria 5, 11 and 12 as its own. Everything after it must land together: the delta publish flips `encoded`, which grep and glean both trust, so a landing with the gram leg reading the live set and the lexical leg not would make every refreshed entry disappear from glean (not in the overlay because `encoded`, not in the index because its lexical rows are in a segment glean does not read). Never cut between the gram and the lexical legs, and never expose the verb before both read the live set.

## Scale and the production posture

- The delta build scans `~encoded ∧ chunked ∧ indexable ∧ live` by surrogate id with `yield_per`, served by `ix_vfs_entries_encoded_kind`, and feeds byte-bounded batches; memory is the Rust accumulator's fixed slot table plus postings proportional to the dirty set; the Design says so (READ, INFERRED).
- The `lex_df` fetch for the builder's vocabulary grows with the dirty set's distinct terms and is chunked by the membership budget: 100,000 terms are 100 statements on Oracle (INFERRED).
- The `lex_docs` delete grows with the dirty set and is chunked by `membership_budget - |S|` under the `(epoch, entry_id)` index (READ).
- `_flip_flags` over the covered pairs is the existing set-based, budgeted flip (`indexing.py:662`) (READ).
- Every reader statement that carries `epoch IN (S)` grows with `|S|`, the number of deltas since the last full publish; the spec bounds the chunk and not `|S|`, and `|S|` grows with refresh cadence times the gap between full rebuilds, so a failed rebuild over a weekend at five-minute cadence passes Oracle's 1,000-element cap: H3.
- The kernel groups grow as (AND-groups × `|S|`) and the lexical head rows as (terms × `HEAD_BLOCKS` × `|S|`); both are in-memory and proportional to `|S|`, named in the memo's risks (READ).
- A no-op refresh is undecided; if it mints a segment, `|S|` grows with every scheduled run whether or not anything changed: H2.
- The live-set read is one statement with one bind returning `|S|` rows (READ).
- The write path is untouched; criterion 2 counts it (READ).

## Premortem

Built as written, a nightly full rebuild fails on a Friday, the five-minute refresh keeps running, and by Monday the live set holds more epochs than Oracle's `IN` list admits; every grep and glean on that engine fails with ORA-01795 until someone runs `reindex` by hand. That is H3.

Built as written, the overnight no-op refreshes each mint an empty segment, because nobody decided otherwise; by morning `|S|` is a hundred, every query carries a hundred-element `IN` list and a hundred head-row fetches per term, and the five-minute cadence the memo priced at 49 segments is a different system. That is H2.

Built as written, a reindex runner is paused for six minutes at the end of its hour-long build, a refresh takes the lease, publishes, and the runner resumes and publishes over it; one file written during the build is `encoded` with its postings in a segment nobody reads, and the lost match is found weeks later by a user. That is H1.

## Findings, most severe first

1. **H1. The full publish rests on the lease to exclude a delta that published mid-build** ([Decisions, 14 and 20](../specifications/active/2026-10-31-refresh-the-incremental-index-verb.md#decisions)) — decision 14's CAS is on the pointer alone because "a full epoch covers a superset of every delta", which is false for a delta published between the full's build commit and its publish; the spec excludes that by decision 20's "no refresh runs" while a full runs, but `claim_reindex_lease` takes a lease five minutes stale (`indexing.py:162`), the module docstring calls the lease an arbiter and not a guard, and `_gram_phases` reads `lost` only between phases (`backend.py:1392`, `1400`). Consequence if built: under a lease lapse, entries the delta covered are `encoded` with their only postings in a segment outside the live set; a lost match in grep and glean until the next full rebuild, with no record saying so. Fix text for decision 14: "The full publish compare-and-sets the pointer and the generation together: `UPDATE vfs_meta SET current_gram_epoch = :epoch, index_generation = index_generation + 1 WHERE id = 1 AND current_gram_epoch = :previous AND index_generation = :seen`, where `:seen` was read in the same statement as the pointer at the start of `build_epoch`; a miss classifies `conflict` and the built epoch is reclaimed, exactly as a lost pointer race is today. A delta can publish during a full only when the lease has lapsed, so the predicate never fires in the normal flow; it is the guard the lease is not."
