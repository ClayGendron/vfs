## The question

A posture change commits its posture row and a mark, then rewrites the stored labels beneath it in chunks. While the mark stands, every reader must judge the rows under the new posture without trusting their labels, and today it does so by compiling the changed row's region, its cover less the covers of every posture row beneath it, into its own rights. The region's description is as large as the number of posture rows beneath the change. The choice is how to keep a reader's compile, and its statements, sized by the reader during that window without ever revealing a row the old or the new posture hides: by judging the region in SQL with one range and a per-row test, by keeping the old posture in force until the relabel is done, by making the window close on its own after a failure, by a coarse hole for a narrowing, or by a combination. It cannot be chosen until the experiment below has run, because each direction moves the cost to a different place (the engine's per-row work, a delayed narrowing, a hidden latency on an unrelated call, a lost read) and none has been measured.

## What is known today

1. READ, what "in flight" means. `posture` writes the posture row and a mark row in `vfs_relabels` in one transaction under the admin lock (`labels.py:200`, `mark_relabel`), so the new posture is in force from that commit. The verb then drives `Relabeller.step` in a loop, one writer transaction per chunk, each rewriting at most `relabel_rows` entry rows (50,000 on every profile, `dialects.py:97`) and at most `in_list_budget` pieces, moving the mark's cursor, and the last chunk deletes the mark and bumps the revision (`labels.py:244-296`, `backend.py:1337-1364`). Between the first commit and the last chunk the store is in flight: some labels beneath are rewritten and some are not.

2. READ, why the compile takes this shape. While a mark stands, `resolve_authority` reads the marks and `inflight_postures`, every posture row at the marked path and every one beneath it (`rights.py:380-385`, `labels.py:221-236`), and `resolve` compiles each pending row's region, `subtract(cover(prefix), cover_all(children))`, as spans when the new level reaches the rung being compiled and as holes when it does not (`grants.py:369-387`, `:595-643`). Inside a hole a row's label is ignored and only the caller's own spans or the owner floor admit it (`grants.py:485-498`). The region must stop at every deeper posture row, because a row under a deeper posture keeps that posture's level and its label, which the relabel does not touch, is already right. That is the guarantee the shape keeps: a row in the region is judged by the new posture, never by a stale label; a row under a deeper posture is judged by its own posture; so no row is revealed that either the old or the new posture hides, at any point of the relabel. Its price is that the region is described piece by piece around every deeper posture row: about three pieces each.

3. EXECUTED (`probe_window.py`, output `probe_window.out`). The world of the scale-pins spec: root posture `read`, one home per user at posture `none` with the user's own `read_write` row, 100 shared folders at posture `none`, 50 groups. With `/home` narrowed from `read` to `list` and held in flight (the posture row and mark written, no label rewritten, the suite's own `_hold_posture` shape), ann, an ordinary caller in five groups, compiles 3,014 hole pieces at the `read` rung at 1,000 users and 30,014 at 10,000; outside the window she compiles none at either size. Her uncached compile issues 9 statements against 7, three of them reading `vfs_grants`, among them the read of every posture row beneath `/home` (READ, `labels.py:474-485`). Her `grep` issues 14 statements at 1,000 users and 81 at 10,000, against 7 at both sizes outside the window; her `glob /shared/**` 49 and 409, against 9; her `read` of her own file 4, unchanged. Every statement stayed bounded, at most 925 binds, and every answer was the steady answer: 167 rows for `grep`, 198 for the glob.

4. EXECUTED (`probe_floors.py`, output `probe_floors.out`), the same shape without a world. A caller holding one grant, while `/` is in flight above D posture rows at `none`: narrowing `/` to `list` gives 302 hole pieces at D = 100, 3,002 at 1,000 and 30,002 at 10,000; widening it gives 201, 2,001 and 20,001 spans. On every range-source engine the hole travels as one bound value, 8,725, 87,025 and 870,025 bytes on SQLite's dialect; the scan tier fans out to 1, 8 and 75 statements per arm chunk (85 on Oracle and the floor), because it spells rights as a literal `OR` on every engine (`scope.py:330-334`, `rights.py:1270-1289`). Linear in D, every count; flat in the caller's own grants.

5. EXECUTED (`probe_generic_hole.py`, output `probe_generic_hole.out`). On the generic floor, which declares no range source, the road's label test spells the hole as one literal `NOT (… OR …)` (`ranges.py:137-158`, `rights.py:1339`), so at 400 posture rows beneath an in-flight `/` an `ls` fails `unavailable`, "Expression tree is too large (maximum depth 1000)", where SQLite's own spelling answers the road. The scale-pins spec fixes that one statement (its decision 11) by bounded tests; the window's growth in statement count stays.

6. EXECUTED (`probe_stuck.py`, output `probe_stuck.out`), how a store gets stuck and what clears it. On a copy of the 1,000-user world, `Relabeller.step` patched to raise a driver error on its first call, the suite's own failure double: `posture("/home", "list")` answered `success` false with one error, `vfs.unavailable`, `retryable` true, carrying the posture row it wrote (`/home`, `list`) and no `relabel` record. The mark stood in `vfs_relabels`, and it still stood after the storage was closed and reopened. ann's `grep` answered its 167 rows at 14 statements while it stood. Then an unrelated `posture("/shared/s000", "none")`, setting the level that folder already had, succeeded and drove the pending relabel to its end; the marks table was empty and ann's `grep` was back to 7 statements.

7. READ, what a caller and an operator see while stuck, and what clears it. A reader sees nothing: its answers are right and carry no record, and no verb, result or log line reports a standing mark (`vfs_relabels` is read only in `labels.py`). The caller of the failed `posture` sees one retryable `unavailable` error and the grant it wrote. The only driver of a relabel is `_settled`, the tail of the `posture` verb (`backend.py:1346`, the one construction of a `Relabeller` in `src/`), and it runs every pending mark to its end, so the state is cleared by the next successful `posture` call on any path, by the same caller retrying or by anyone. `rebuild_labels` (`labels.py:388`) also clears every mark, but nothing in `src/` calls it; no verb, no rebuild pass and no first touch does. INFERRED: a process that dies between two chunks leaves the same state as a failed chunk.

8. EXECUTED (`probe_relabel.py`, output `probe_relabel.out`), how long the window lasts in transactions. With R rows strictly beneath the posture path and no posture row beneath it, a relabel takes one transaction when `R + 1 ≤ relabel_rows`, else `2 + floor(R / (relabel_rows − 1))`: 4 transactions for 100,000 files at the real cap. A read between two chunks, through the `relabel:after-chunk` seam, answered exactly what the pointwise oracle says at every firing. With posture rows beneath, a chunk is also capped by `in_list_budget` pieces, so a region cut by D posture rows takes at least `ceil(3D / in_list_budget)` transactions: at D = 10,000, 31 on Oracle and the floor, 16 on SQL Server and 1 on SQLite, however few rows it rewrites (INFERRED from `labels.py:438-466` and item 4).

9. READ and INFERRED, the window's cost at [the reference deployment](../../AGENTS.md). The multiplier is per verb call: about 150,000 a day, 5 a second over an eight-hour day and 20 at peak, and almost every call is a partial caller's. The window's length follows the rows the relabel rewrites; its per-call cost follows the posture rows beneath it. Relabel rates, READ: SQL Server 38 µs a row (`dialects.py:322`), Postgres about 16 µs and SQLite about 1.4 µs (the 2026-10-02 memo, §2.4, 16.0 s and 1.43 s a million rows in 50,000-row chunks). A 100,000-row relabel holds the window for about 3.8 s on SQL Server, 1.6 s on Postgres and 0.14 s on SQLite: about 76, 32 and 3 calls at peak. If the change sits above the deployment's 1,000 homes as posture rows (the deployment's posture-row count is an assumption; the 1,000-user world stands for it), each of those calls pays about what item 3 measured at 1,000 users: twice the `grep` statements, five times the `glob` statements, a cold compile reading 1,000 posture rows once per caller. A change at the root over the 10,000,000-file estate with R of about 9,000,000 rows holds it for about 182 transactions: about 5.7 minutes on SQL Server, 2.4 on Postgres, 13 s on SQLite, about 6,800 calls at peak on SQL Server. A stuck window lasts until the next `posture` call, which may be days: at 150,000 calls a day every partial read pays the window's factor for all of them, two to five times the statements at 1,000 posture rows and twelve to forty-five times at 10,000. Answers stay right throughout; only cost grows.

## Candidate directions

None is chosen here. Each is stated with what it would cost and what it risks.

1. One range and a per-row test in SQL. The compile carries the pending row's cover as one range, the same at any D, and the statement asks per row, in SQL, whether a deeper posture row covers it: a correlated probe of `vfs_grants` through the `(principal_id, path_prefix)` key for the deepest everyone row at or above the row's path beneath the pending prefix. Cost: one index probe per row the statement visits inside the region, on every engine, for the window only; a new statement shape per dialect and its plan pins. Risk: the deepest-covering-prefix test is not a single range seek, and an engine that plans it as a join over every posture row brings D back into the work instead of the text.

2. The old posture stays the single truth until the relabel completes. The relabel writes the new level into a second column (or a revision-keyed label), and one final statement makes it authoritative; readers judge by the old labels, which are consistent, so no window exists. Cost: a schema change, a second write per row, one switch statement that must be atomic with the posture row. Risk: a narrowing is not in force until the relabel finishes, minutes at the deployment's root; that is a security delay, a change to what `posture` promises today ("in force from then on", `backend.py:1024`).

3. A failed relabel resumes on its own. The window's cost is unchanged, but its length is bounded by the relabel rather than by the next `posture` call: a driver on first touch, on any admin write, on the index pass, or on a background tick under the admin lock. Cost: the driver's lock and lease, and a resume on a call that did not ask for one. Risk: a hidden latency on an unrelated call, a relabel racing a rival admin write (the planner already remakes its plan on a moved revision, `labels.py:281-283`).

4. A coarse hole for a narrowing. A narrowing in flight compiles the whole cover of the pending row as one hole, deeper postures included, so the caller's compile is two pieces; a widening keeps today's exact region, since a coarse widening would reveal rows under deeper, narrower postures. Cost: none in the compile. Risk: during the window a row under a deeper, more open posture is hidden from callers who hold it only by the label: a wrong answer, never a leak, for the window's length.

5. Refusing a posture change above a declared number of posture rows would bound the window by refusing the change, and is named here only to set it aside: it is a designed scale cap, which [the production posture](../../AGENTS.md) rules out.

## One line

While a posture change is in flight every partial caller compiles every posture row beneath it, so with 10,000 homes beneath `/home` an ordinary caller's `grep` costs 81 statements instead of 7 and its `glob` 409 instead of 9, with every answer right; a failed chunk keeps it so until the next `posture` call on any path, and judging the region by one range in SQL, holding the old posture until done, resuming on its own, or a coarse narrowing hole each move that cost somewhere the experiment above must measure.
