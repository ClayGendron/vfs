## The question

A listing names, as a bare directory, each hidden child directory that has a row the caller may name beneath it: the *road*, so an agent can walk down to what it may read. Today `ls` and `glob` find the road by asking about every hidden child directory they fetched, in statements that each carry a chunk of those candidates, once for every 50 pieces of the caller's owner arm and once more for the label. The choice is how to find the same road at a cost that follows what the caller may see rather than what the directory holds: fewer statements carrying every test at once, a road found from the caller's own rows the way `tree` already finds it, the rights pushed into the children fetch so hidden children are never read, or today's shape kept because the deployment's directories are small. It cannot be chosen until the experiment below has run, because each moves the cost to a different place and none has been measured on a server engine.

## What is known today

1. READ, how `ls` finds the road. `ls` fetches every child of the listed directory by `parent_id`, with no rights term, and judges each in Python (`reads.py:1083-1136`); the hidden children that are directories go to `Visibility.road` (`reads.py:1129`, `rights.py:615-643`). The road has three sources at the `list` rung. The caller's own roots' ancestors, one chunked lookup sized by the caller (`rights.py:673-679`). For each member's owner arm, `_owned_beneath` (`rights.py:1303-1321`): its pieces in groups of 50, each group one `EXISTS` test, run over chunks of `B − 103` candidates, where B is the membership budget. Then `_open_beneath` (`rights.py:1324-1341`): one label test over chunks of `B − 3`. Every test runs as `SELECT d.path FROM vfs_entries AS d WHERE d.path IN (<chunk>) AND EXISTS (<a row beneath d passing the test>)` (`rights.py:1344-1369`). `glob` does the same for the hidden directories among its pattern's candidates (`reads.py:446`), which it also fetches with no rights term (`reads.py:321`).

2. EXECUTED (`probe_compile.py` and `probe_statements.py`, outputs `probe_compile.out` and `probe_statements.out`). The scale-pins world: root posture `read`, one home per user at posture `none` with the user's own `read_write` row, 100 shared folders, 50 groups; ann is an ordinary caller in five groups with 34 covered prefixes and 104 owner pieces; heavy is in all 50 groups, 305 owner pieces. On SQLite's own budget (B = 32,668), ann's `ls /home` issues 10 statements at 1,000 users and at 10,000, and answers one row, her home. The four road statements carry every other home as a candidate: the widest grows from 1,106 binds and 13,290 bound bytes at 1,000 users to 10,106 binds and 121,290 bytes at 10,000. heavy's issues 14 at both sizes. The children fetch reads every child of `/home` either way (READ: its text carries no rights term and is the system actor's own), 1,004 and 10,004 rows.

3. EXECUTED (`probe_budget.py`, output `probe_budget.out`), under Oracle's 1,000-element `IN` list (SQLite's profile with `in_list_budget` 1,000, so B = 1,000). ann's `ls /home` issues 14 statements at 1,000 users and 53 at 10,000; heavy's 22 and 101; ann's `glob /home/**` 13 and 52, heavy's 21 and 100. Every statement stays within 1,000 binds. Every other verb probed (`ls /`, `ls /shared`, `glob /shared/**`, `grep`, `read`, a write) issued the same count at both sizes.

4. EXECUTED, the law. The road's statements number exactly `ceil(P / 50) × ceil(H / (B − 103)) + ceil(H / (B − 3))`, with H the hidden child directories, P the caller's owner pieces at `list` and B the membership budget; the listing adds six more for a cached caller. ann at 1,000 users under B = 1,000: P = 104 gives 3 owner tests, H = 1,003 gives 2 chunks each, `3 × 2 + 2 = 8`, plus six, 14; at 10,000, `3 × 12 + 11 = 47`, plus six, 53. heavy: P = 305, 7 tests, `7 × 2 + 2 = 16` and `7 × 12 + 11 = 95`, plus six, 22 and 101.

5. EXECUTED (`probe_floors.py`, output `probe_floors.out`), how P grows with the caller's own grants. For a single member whose c covered prefixes are disjoint siblings, the owner arm holds `3c + 2` pieces, so the road runs `ceil((3c + 2) / 50)` owner tests per candidate chunk: 3 at 34 direct grants, 7 at 100, 31 at 500, 61 at 1,000, 301 at 5,000, 1,201 at 20,000; and 13, 67 and 307 for a member of 50 groups holding 2, 20 and 100 grants each plus 100 direct.

6. READ, by engine. B is `min(in_list_budget, parameter budget − 32)` (`dialects.py:735-742`): 32,668 on SQLite, Postgres and MariaDB, 2,000 on SQL Server, 1,000 on Oracle and the generic floor. So on the first three the candidates rarely split and the statement count is `ceil(P / 50) + 1`, flat, while each statement's candidate list grows with H; on SQL Server and Oracle the count grows with H too. SQL Server sends each chunk as a `VALUES` derived table rather than an `IN` list (`membership.py:54-59`), the same chunking.

7. EXECUTED (`probe_compile.py`), the contrast. `tree /home` issues 5 statements at 1,000 and at 10,000 users for ann and for heavy, one of them the range join of the visible set, because `tree` finds its road from the rows the caller may see (their hidden ancestors inside the tree), not by asking about each hidden child.

8. INFERRED, the engine's work. Each `EXISTS` that finds no match must look at the rows beneath its candidate before it can answer no, unless the planner drives it from another index (`owner_id` for the owner tests, `(everyone_level, path)` for the label test). In this world every home holds two files, so one `ls /home` by ann may visit every row under `/home` once per test: about 4 × 20,000 rows at 10,000 users. The plan each engine takes is not measured.

9. READ and INFERRED, the multiplier at [the reference deployment](../../AGENTS.md). It is per verb call, about 150,000 a day, most of them reads, lists, globs and greps; but only a listing or glob of a directory whose child directories are hidden from its caller pays, and the deployment's median directory holds two files, a quarter none. The tail is where it bites: at its largest measured directory, 1,533 entries, all of them hidden child directories, an ordinary caller like ann pays 8 road statements on Oracle and 4 on SQL Server and SQLite; a caller with 1,000 grants of its own (61 owner tests) pays 124 on Oracle and 62 on SQL Server. A home directory for the deployment's 1,000 people costs ann 8 on Oracle and heavy 16. Each statement stays inside its engine's caps; what grows is the number of statements, the length of each candidate list, and the engine work of item 8, while the answer is the caller's own few rows.

## Candidate directions

None is chosen here. Each is stated with what it would cost and what it risks.

1. Every test in one `EXISTS` per candidate chunk. The owner tests and the label test become one `OR` inside one subquery, so the count is `ceil(H / chunk)`. Cost: the pieces share the statement with the candidates, so the chunk shrinks as P grows and a caller with many grants splits again. Risk: one wider `OR` per candidate may plan worse than several narrow ones, and the depth budget caps the pieces one statement can carry.

2. The road found from the caller's own rows, as `tree` finds it. Read the rows beneath the listed directory that the caller may name, its roots already known, its owned rows through the `owner_id` index, the rows visible by label through `(everyone_level, path)`, and take each one's ancestor one level beneath the listed directory. Cost: follows the rows the caller may name beneath the directory, not its hidden children. Risk: an open estate with many visible rows beneath a directory reads many rows to find a few children, unless the engine can skip to the next child (a skip scan or a grouped seek per child), which each engine plans in its own way.

3. The rights pushed into the children fetch. `ls` fetches only the children the caller may see, through the range join `tree` uses, plus the road of direction 2, so a hidden child is never read. Cost: a new listing statement per dialect and its plan pins. Risk: the listing's ordering and paging rules (the listing cursor's spec) must hold on the joined statement.

4. Today's shape, kept. The cost follows the listed directory's size, which the deployment's median keeps tiny, and every statement is bounded. Cost: nothing now. Risk: the tail, a shared `/home` or a mount root with thousands of private children, pays per call on the engines with small budgets, and the work of item 8 grows with every row beneath.

## One line

A partial caller's `ls /home` asks the road about every other user's home, `ceil(P / 50) × ceil(H / (B − 103)) + ceil(H / (B − 3))` statements, 14 growing to 53 on Oracle's budget between 1,000 and 10,000 users for one row of answer, while `tree` finds the same road in one visibility statement; merging the tests, finding the road from the caller's own rows, or pushing the rights into the fetch each trade that for a cost the experiment above must measure.
