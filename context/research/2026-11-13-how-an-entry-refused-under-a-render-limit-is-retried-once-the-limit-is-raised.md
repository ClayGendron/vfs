## The question

A render limit is a per-render cap a host sets through `RenderLimits`: the character budget, the unit cap, the clock, the embedded caps, and, once the office-container spec lands, the three container limits. An entry whose rendering a limit cut or refused keeps that verdict on its row when the host later raises the limit, because the verdict's stamp names the bytes and the renderer, never the limits. Which entries should a raise render again, by what mechanism, at what cost per raise, and without disturbing the stamp and twin laws the render stage keeps today?

## What is known today

1. READ, the stamp. The render stage selects a bytes row only when its `render_generation` or `render_source_hash` is absent or misses (`src/vfs/storage/backends/database/render.py:150-177`). The generation is the renderer's name and its version, which is its `revision` and its library's installed version (`src/vfs/rendering/seam.py:165-172`, `:282-285`). No field of `RenderLimits` enters either.
2. READ, the twins. A body whose bytes hash and generation match a settled row borrows that row's verdict, a failure state included, so a corrupt or refused file copied many times is rendered once (`render.py:180-240`). A copy of a refused body under another path is therefore refused without a render, and a write of the same bytes keeps the same hash.
3. READ, the retry rule as specified. [Binary bodies and text renderings](../specifications/active/152-binary-bodies-and-text-renderings/spec.md), section 6, says the same bytes under the same generation are not retried for `unsupported`, `unavailable`, `encrypted`, `corrupt` or `failed`, and that a generation bump, an extra install or a new write retries (`spec.md:90`).
4. READ, which limits a host may raise. `RenderLimits` refuses a non-positive field and a raised output `ratio`, and nothing else (`seam.py:66-84`), so `chars`, `pages`, `seconds`, `embedded_depth` and `embedded_count` may be raised today; the office-container spec adds `container_members`, `container_bytes` and `container_ratio`, also raisable (its decision 10). The value is given once, when the backend is built (`src/vfs/storage/backends/database/backend.py:233`, `:267`).
5. READ, which verdicts a raise could change. The Builder writes `truncated` when the character budget or the unit cap cut the text, `partial` when the clock or the embedded count cut it, and `failed` for the output ratio guard, which may not be raised (`src/vfs/rendering/markdown.py:228-260`). After the office-container spec, a container refused by its member count, declared total or ratio, or not verified within the clock, is `failed` with a detail naming the limit; a container the formats do not admit is `corrupt`, which no limit changes (its decisions 9 and 16). So the verdicts a raise could change are `truncated`, `partial` and some `failed`, and the status alone does not say which limit produced a `failed` row; its `render_detail` does, in words.
6. READ, what reports the verdicts. The reindex result counts `failed` and `corrupt` together under `failed` (`render.py:116-129`); a caller's read reports a row with no text as `unsupported`, not retryable, with `render_status` in its data (`src/vfs/storage/backends/database/reads.py:875-910`); `render_detail` is a row-only column (`src/vfs/models/rows.py:90`).
7. INFERRED from items 1 to 5 and [the reference deployment](../../AGENTS.md). Folding every limit into the generation would make any change of any limit re-dirty every bytes entry of every renderer, the corpus multiplier of 10,000,000 files, where a raise needs to touch only the entries a lower limit cut or refused. Keying a retry on the status would re-render every `failed` row, including the output-ratio failures a raise cannot change, unless the row records which limit, at which value, produced it.
8. INFERRED by the spec review's re-read of the office-container spec (its finding M3), recorded here on 2026-11-13 as a second coupling of the same question, what the index does with a render that never reports: a render worker killed for memory lands no verdict, so its row keeps a stamp that misses and is selected again on every index pass, and is rendered, and killed, again. Its basis is the selection of item 1, which picks every row whose stamp is absent or misses (`render.py:150-177`), and the fact that no clock interrupts a library's open (the office-container spec's decision 12), so a render that exhausts its worker's memory ends without a `Rendering` to land. It predates the office-container spec, which lowers how much one docx or pptx render can hold and does not change this path. Nothing here was executed, and nothing is decided.

## The experiment that answers it

The research is done when each item below has a labelled result.

1. On a generated store of 10,000 and 100,000 bytes entries, with a known share cut or refused under each limit, prototype three mechanisms in a scratch copy of the tree: (a) the limits a renderer's verdicts depend on folded into its generation; (b) a row stamped with the limit and the value that cut or refused it, and selected again when the live limit exceeds that value; (c) an operator option of the index pass that re-dirties the rows whose status a named limit produces. For each, the rows a raise of one limit selects, against the rows that limit actually cut or refused.
2. For each mechanism, the statements and row visits a raise costs, and what it adds to every render and every index pass when no limit changes, measured on both stores and projected at the reference deployment's 10,000,000 files on the per-index-visit multiplier.
3. For each, how twin borrowing behaves: whether a body refused under the lower limit can lend its verdict to a twin rendered after the raise, counted on a store holding copies.
4. For each, what a lowered limit does, since a host may lower as well as raise: whether rows rendered under the higher value are left, re-rendered or marked.
5. For the coupling of item 8: on a scratch build whose render worker is stopped mid-render, the rows left unstamped, how often each later pass selects them again, and what each mechanism of item 1 would record for a render that never reports, counted, not designed.
6. What the following spec needs from this memo: the selection each mechanism makes, its schema change if any, its cost per raise and per pass, and its interaction with the twin and skip laws. The choice is that spec's.

## One line

A render limit enters neither the bytes hash nor the generation that stamp a rendering, so raising a limit renders nothing again until the bytes or the renderer change; the research is to cost three ways a raise could select exactly what the lower limit cut or refused.
