## The question

A stage that filters rows by the glob gates does two things. It judges: every row gets a verdict against the call's gates, in one hop on the process pool. It admits: each row is kept or dropped by its verdict and its own facts. The first half has one owner. The second half, and the code that joins the two, is written out again at each site. Can one function serve every site, where would it live so that each site imports it downward, and what would it cost each site to call it?

## What is known today

1. READ, vfs at `b2a32c3`. Three parts already have one owner each. The hop is `judge_rows` in `src/vfs/offload.py`: it takes an executor, the gates, a sequence of `(path, name)` subjects and a deadline, and returns one verdict per subject and whether every row was judged. The filter law is `GlobGates.admits` in `src/vfs/pattern_matching/glob.py`: a verdict and an extension in, a boolean out. The storage row gate is `passes_gates` in `src/vfs/storage/backends/database/scope.py`: it adds the tombstone side and the meta-subtree rule to `admits` for a stored row.
2. EXECUTED on 2026-11-12, the same commit: `grep -rn 'judge_rows(' src/vfs` lists seven lines. One is the definition, one is the worked example in the docstring of `src/vfs/pattern_matching/glob.py`, and five are calls. Each call is followed by its own admission loop. Those five are the spellings.
3. READ. Site one is `AsyncVirtualFileSystem._judged` in `src/vfs/base.py`. Its rows are `Observation`s the router holds. It builds each subject from the row's path and name, admits by `gates.admits(verdict, extract_extension(row.path))`, applies no tombstone side and no meta rule (what a caller holds is never hidden), makes no hop when there are no rows or no pattern, and returns the kept rows with the completed flag. It has three callers: chained glob, chained grep and the root probe. The router spec moves it to `src/vfs/chain.py` as `judge_in_hand` and changes nothing in it.
4. READ. Site two is inline in `AsyncVirtualFileSystem._grep_dispatches` in `src/vfs/base.py`. Its rows are scope roots, as paths. It admits by `gates.admits(verdict, None)`: the gates hold the caller's `globs` alone and no extension is consulted. An unfinished hop appends one truncation record. The review of 2026-11-07 counted four spellings and did not count this one.
5. READ. Site three is `_gated` in `src/vfs/storage/backends/database/grep.py`. Its rows are row mappings. It builds each subject from the stored `path` and `name` columns, admits by `passes_gates`, and passes every row unjudged when the call is ungated. It has two call sites in that module.
6. READ. Site four is `_Gates.judge` with `_Gates.admits` in `src/vfs/storage/backends/database/search.py`. Its rows are chunk rows, several to an entry, so it judges each distinct entry once and returns a mapping from entry to verdict, not a list of kept rows. The admission happens later, at three places (`search_rows`, `_entries_for_chunks` and `passes`), each joined with the rights view's own test.
7. READ. Site five is inline in `glob_rows` in `src/vfs/storage/backends/database/reads.py`. It runs once per tombstone side with that side's own gates, admits by `gates.admits(verdict, mapping["ext"])` directly, and then sorts each admitted row into served, or hidden behind the rights view.
8. INFERRED. The five differ in five ways: the row type (an observation, a path, a row mapping, a chunk row keyed by entry); where the extension comes from (derived from the path, read from a column, or not consulted); the rule added to the filter law (none, or the tombstone side and the meta rule); what comes back (kept rows, or verdicts for a later admission); and what follows (nothing, a truncation record, or the rights view).
9. INFERRED, from the ladder in `tests/lint/test_code_shape.py`. After the router spec lands the five sit in five modules: `vfs.chain` and the three adapter modules on the rung beneath the router, and `vfs.base` on the router's rung. A function all five import must sit beneath all of them, on the seam's rung or lower. `vfs.offload`, where the hop lives, is one rung beneath the seam; `vfs.pattern_matching` is lower still and may not import the hop.

## The experiment that answers it

The research is done when each item below has a labelled result.

1. A table of the five sites: the row type, how the subject is built, the admission predicate, the rule it adds, what it returns, and the number of callers. The result is how many of the five differ only in a parameter a shared function could take.
2. One candidate function in a scratch copy of the tree, beside `judge_rows`, taking the gates, the rows, a function from a row to its subject and a function from a row and its verdict to a boolean. Each site is replaced by a call to it where that changes no answer. The result is the number of sites replaced, the lines removed, the faults `test_imports_point_down_the_ladder` reports, and the failing nodes of `uv run pytest tests/base tests/storage tests/test_offload.py tests/test_chain.py`.
3. A count of `judge_rows` calls and of subjects judged per verb call, before and after, for one chained glob, one scoped grep and one ranked search over rows with several chunks to an entry, by a spy on `judge_rows`. The ranked search judges each entry once today; the result says whether a shared function keeps that.
4. What the following spec needs from this memo: the table, the counts of items 2 and 3, and the sites a shared function cannot serve without changing what they return. The choice is that spec's.
