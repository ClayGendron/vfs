# A top bar of four to seven entries on one axis, with a sidebar that shows only the chosen entry's pages, is the common shape; single-product sites split the bar by content type and multi-product companies split it by product, so vfs should put about five content-type tabs in the bar and its domains in the sidebar

I was asked, on 2026-11-12, how documentation sites combine a horizontal bar of sections with a left sidebar: first the sites built on Material for MkDocs with its tabs feature, then sites on other tools, and what that means for the vfs site. The time box was 30 minutes. I used about 30 of them. I read nineteen sites. None was skipped. Two had changed since they were commonly described: Pydantic's docs now run on Astro Starlight instead of Material, and Polars now builds with Zensical, Material's successor.

## How I read the sites

EXECUTED: for the seven Material-family sites I downloaded each public navigation file with curl and ran a short script that prints the top two levels and counts the pages under each top entry. EXECUTED: for every live site I downloaded the HTML with a generic browser user agent and printed the visible text of the header and of each `nav` element. I did this because WebFetch returned only the page body, without navigation, for Docker, React, Next.js and Polars. READ: Mintlify's `docs.json` gave its exact tab list. INFERRED: a page count from a navigation file is exact for the pages listed there; a count from a live page counts sidebar links and is an estimate of the section's size.

A term used throughout. A **tab** here means one entry in the horizontal bar that, when chosen, changes what the sidebar shows. A **scoped sidebar** is one that shows only the pages under the chosen tab. A **landing page** is the page a tab or a section opens on.

## What Material's navigation features do

READ (Material's navigation setup page): `navigation.tabs` renders the top-level entries of the `nav` as a bar below the header, on screens wider than 1220 pixels. Below that width the tabs are not shown as a bar; the top-level entries "remain as-is" in the slide-out drawer. `navigation.tabs.sticky` keeps the bar visible while the reader scrolls. `navigation.sections` renders top-level entries as visible groups in the sidebar instead of collapsible folders; with tabs on, it applies one level down, so the second-level entries under the current tab become sidebar groups. `navigation.indexes` lets an `index.md` be the landing page of its section, so clicking the section's name opens a page instead of only expanding a folder; it is incompatible with `toc.integrate`. `navigation.path` draws breadcrumbs above the title, which the page says helps on small screens; it is marked experimental. `navigation.prune` emits only the visible part of the navigation into each page's HTML, cutting site size by a third or more, and turns collapsed sections into links; it is experimental and incompatible with `navigation.expand`. `toc.integrate` moves the page's table of contents into the left sidebar. READ: the same page carries a banner that names Zensical as Material's "modern successor".

EXECUTED: the live FastAPI, Textual, Material and Polars pages all carry the class `md-nav--lifted` on the primary sidebar. INFERRED: that is how Material scopes the sidebar to the active tab on a wide screen; the drawer on a phone still holds the whole tree, with the tab names as its top level. INFERRED: the consequence for vfs is that tab names must also read well as the top level of a phone's menu.

## The pattern

INFERRED from the table: the common count of bar entries is four to seven. Docker, React, Svelte's products, Mintlify, Supabase, FastAPI and Material all fall in that range. The outliers pay for it. Textual's eleven include five one-page tabs that leave the sidebar empty. Stripe's ten need a "More" overflow that hides entries. Pydantic's nine were abandoned when the site moved tools. Polars's two spend the bar on a product exit.

INFERRED: the bar's axis follows the company, not the framework. A single product splits the bar by content type: Docker, React, Textual, Mintlify, FastAPI and Material do, with the Diátaxis types renamed (Get started, Learn, Guides, Manuals, Setup, Reference). A company with several products splits the bar by product and puts content types in the sidebar: Pydantic today, Polars, Svelte, Stripe, HashiCorp. Pydantic is the clearest case, because it made exactly that move once it had five products.

INFERRED: domains, the areas of one product, almost always live in the sidebar, as groups inside a tab. Mintlify (Create content, Search and SEO, Integrations), Material (Setup's pages by feature), Supabase (each product's sidebar) and Tailwind (property families) all do this. No single-product site I read puts its domains on the bar.

INFERRED: when the bar is used, the sidebar follows it. Every site with a docs-level bar scopes the sidebar to the chosen entry; only Kubernetes and Vercel keep one long sidebar, and both use their bar for site areas instead. Every bar entry opens on a landing page, which in Material needs `navigation.indexes`.

INFERRED: second dimensions that are not content (version, router, API style, language) go either beside the logo (React, Tailwind, Kubernetes) or at the top of the sidebar (Next.js, Vue). None of the sites puts them on the content bar. Search sits in the header on every site I read except Vercel, where it heads the sidebar. The repository link sits at the right of the header.

INFERRED: on a phone the bar disappears. Material hides tabs below 1220 pixels and shows the tab names as the drawer's top level; Docker hides its tab list below its medium breakpoint. So the bar is a wide-screen shortcut to the first level of the tree, not a separate navigation.

## What the vfs site should do

**The bar and the sidebar.** INFERRED: turn on `navigation.tabs`, `navigation.sections`, `navigation.indexes` and `navigation.path`, and keep `navigation.tabs.sticky` optional. Put the content types on the bar, in reading order: Get started, Tutorials, How-to guides, Reference, Explanation, so five entries now and at most six or seven later, with a project entry (History, changelog, contributing) as the only addition. The reason is that vfs is one product, and every single-product site I read with a docs-level bar uses the content-type axis, while the product axis belongs to companies with several products (Docker, React, Textual and Mintlify for the first; Pydantic, Svelte and Stripe for the second). Put the domains in the sidebar, as `navigation.sections` groups inside each tab: the file system and its calls, the command line, storage and databases, search, permissions and governance, AI agents and MCP, mounts, operations. Use the same domain names and order in every tab, so a reader who knows "permissions" finds it under Tutorials, How-to guides and Reference alike. This is how Mintlify, Material and Supabase carry a second dimension without a second bar. Give every tab and every domain group an `index.md` landing page, because every site I read opens each entry on one. Add a tab only when it has a landing page and several pages behind it, because Textual's one-page tabs show the cost of a tab that leaves the sidebar empty. Keep search and the GitHub link in the header, as now, and add no version selector until there is a release to select; when there is one, it goes in the header (React, Kubernetes), not the bar. Name the tabs so they also read well as the top of a phone's menu, because that is where Material puts them below 1220 pixels. Until How-to guides, Reference and Explanation have pages, the bar can hold only Get started (Introduction, Overview), Tutorials and History; the bar grows as the types are written.

**The tutorials, now and later.** INFERRED: at five, keep one ordered list, a single path a newcomer follows from Build Your First File System to Control Who Can Access Files, because every site's beginner material I read is a short linear path (React's GET STARTED, Docker's Get started, Material's Getting started, uv's Getting started, FastAPI's Tutorial - User Guide). At thirty, keep a short first path of about five lessons at the top of the Tutorials tab, then group the rest by domain, as sidebar sections named like the domain groups in the other tabs. The reason is that the sites I read separate level only once, at the boundary between a beginner path and everything after it (FastAPI's Tutorial and Advanced User Guide, Docker's Get started tab against its Guides tab, React's Get started against its chapters), and they sort everything after that boundary by area (Docker's Guides, Mintlify's groups, Supabase's products). A label of level can still appear on a page, but the sidebar groups should be domains. This part rests on the navigation shapes only; Researcher 03's question is the one that studies tutorial grouping directly.

In one line: about five content-type tabs on the bar, the product's domains as groups in a sidebar scoped to the chosen tab, a landing page for every entry, and tutorials as one short beginner path followed by domain groups.

## What this study did not settle

Several cells in the table say "not checked" or "not counted", because the live markup did not show a version selector or a page total within the time box; a second pass reading each site's sitemap would count pages and confirm where version selectors sit. I did not measure phone behaviour for the non-Material sites beyond Docker's breakpoint class; loading each site in a browser at phone width would settle what their bars become. I did not test the recommended Material configuration on the vfs site itself; building the current eight pages with tabs, sections and indexes, and viewing them at 1220 pixels and below, would show whether a bar of three entries looks deliberate or thin before the other types exist.
