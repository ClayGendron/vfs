# vfs pins almost every claim it makes on chosen examples or fixed-seed generated worlds, proves none for every input beyond a few closed vocabularies, leaves its Rust tests and its new documentation outside every gate, and four cheap experiments raised three pins a grade while finding one broken law

The box opened at 17:02 PST on 2026-11-12, and the study finished inside it, at about 17:25. This study reads the vfs tree read-only at commit `3ea2465eda842a8d3ec54a6018db474a60198a9d` with the working tree's uncommitted changes as they stood, lists the claims vfs makes about itself that a stranger would rely on, finds what pins each one today, and grades each pin on the brief's five rungs: proven for every input, tested by generated inputs, tested on chosen examples, checked by a lint, or stated and unpinned. A pin is the test, lint or gate that would fail if the claim stopped being true. Four experiments, kept under [experiments/04/](experiments/04/) with their outputs in [outputs.txt](experiments/04/outputs.txt), test whether the cheapest techniques raise a pin. Every claim below carries a label: EXECUTED (a command run here, with its output), READ (a file cited by path) or INFERRED (reasoning from the two).

## 2. Pins on chosen examples: the two conformance contracts and the gate tests

The largest family of pins is hand-written examples, and the best of them are the two shared contracts. `tests/support/storage_contract.py` holds 232 test methods and `tests/support/grants_contract.py` 85 (EXECUTED, `grep -c "def test_"`). Both run through `tests/storage/test_conformance.py` on six classes: in-memory, SQLite, Postgres, MariaDB, SQL Server and Oracle (READ). So one written example becomes six executions, on every engine vfs names as supported. What a pass proves: on those inputs, on those engines, the verb gives that answer. What it does not prove: any input nobody wrote down.

The permission gate (C1) is the strongest example pin, because it is enumerated over a closed vocabulary. `test_read_only_mount_rejects_every_mutation` in `tests/base/test_gates.py` is parametrized over `sorted(MUTATING_OPS)`, and for each op it asserts the result is `read_only` **and** that the recording child storage saw no call at all (`assert child.calls == []  # gated before any dispatch`) (READ). Its twin asserts a writable mount dispatches each mutation exactly once. Because `tests/test_ops.py` pins the router's public surface to `ALL_OPS` with a drift test, a new mutating verb joins this parametrization automatically (READ, INFERRED). That makes C1 proven over every op for one shape of mount (a read-only child under a writable root); per-prefix maps and deeper layerings are pinned on chosen examples (`test_read_only_root_makes_writable_child_read_only_end_to_end`, `test_subtree_verbs_honour_a_read_rule_beneath`) (READ).

Hiding rows from verbs (C2) is pinned verb by verb on chosen examples in `grants_contract.py`: `test_a_hidden_row_reads_exactly_as_a_missing_one`, `test_glob_never_names_a_hidden_row`, `test_grep_never_matches_inside_a_hidden_row` and its index twin, `test_a_hidden_only_term_never_reaches_the_statistics_export`, `test_a_hidden_partition_never_moves_a_visible_score`, and the write-side refusals (READ). Traversal across a hidden node (C4) is pinned by two examples, `test_a_hidden_middle_node_cuts_the_walk_under_the_join` and its clause-fan twin in `tests/storage/database/test_traverse.py` (READ). Trash and restore (C13) are pinned by the storage contract's trash tests and by `tests/storage/database/test_trash.py`, which also counts statements (READ).

The batch-size claim (C5), the one AGENTS.md calls a supported contract, is pinned only by examples. The examples are good ones: `test_tiny_budget_batch_survives_every_verb` squeezes the parameter budget to 48 and drives 60 files through write, stat, edit, glob and ls; `test_wide_pattern_fan_survives_the_expression_depth_cap` sends 1,100 glob arms; on SQL Server, `TestMSSQLBindBudget` writes 1,100 files under 1,100 parents and `TestMSSQLMembershipForm` locks about 2,000 keys; 21 test files hang a `before_cursor_execute` listener to count statements (READ, EXECUTED by grep). What is missing is the universal form. No fixture watches every statement the suite issues and refuses one whose bind count passes the profile's budget, so a new call site that forgets `chunked()` passes every SQLite test (SQLite's in-list budget is 32,766) and fails only on an engine leg whose test happens to push that site past 1,000 keys (READ `dialects.py`, INFERRED).

The unknown-dialect claim (C6) is pinned by two examples that check declarations, not behaviour: `test_unknown_dialect_serves_on_the_generic_floor` asserts `profile_for("duckdb")` returns the floor, and `test_an_unknown_dialect_serves_the_core_verbs_and_withholds_search` asserts the capability set (READ). No conformance class runs the contract on the floor: the six classes in `test_conformance.py` are the five named engines and memory (READ).

The classified-result claim (C10) is pinned by examples at its boundaries: `test_error_never_raises_and_carries_the_op`, `test_hopeless_payload_returns_fatal_internal_never_raises`, `test_hostile_mapping_is_hopeless_never_raises`, `test_resolve_path_never_raises_on_non_str`, and every contract test that asserts an error kind (READ). The universal half of the claim, that no bad input to any verb ever raises, has no pin: nothing feeds arbitrary arguments to the public verbs. The source holds 218 `raise` statements and 15 broad `except Exception` boundaries (EXECUTED, grep), so the claim rests on those boundaries being complete. The `Result` algebra (C11) is pinned on one hand-built example per law in `tests/results/test_result_laws.py` (READ); section 6 shows what that leaves open.

## 3. Pins by generated inputs: seeded worlds and the oracles

vfs has no Hypothesis and no proptest today. `grep` finds zero imports of `hypothesis` and zero `@given` in `tests/`, and no `proptest`, `quickcheck`, `arbitrary` or fuzz target in `crates/` (EXECUTED). What it does have is a hand-rolled form of the same idea: 33 test functions, in 13 files, build a `random.Random(seed)` with a fixed seed and loop over generated cases, plus two helpers that do the same (EXECUTED, by an AST script over `tests/`). The suite holds 3,337 test functions in all (EXECUTED), so about 1% of test functions generate their inputs.

Those 33 are placed where they matter most. The rights resolver (C3) runs 400 seeded worlds a parametrization against the pointwise oracle in `tests/support/oracles/grants.py`, which answers the same question "the slow, obvious way, for one path at a time" (READ `tests/storage/test_grants.py`, `oracles/grants.py`). The database's compiled visibility clauses and range join run 30 worlds against the same oracle on SQLite, under three parameter budgets (4, 9 and 2,099) so the chunked fan is exercised (READ `test_rights.py`). The Rust kernels (C7) are checked the same way: postings builders on 200 generated documents over an alphabet chosen to hold `\x00`, `é`, `ı` and `İ` (`test_fuzz_rows_identical`), the grep ladder, posting selection, the vector top-k at two sizes, the centrality signals on 18 random graphs, the matcher's context windows, and the graph walk and meeting subgraph against their oracles (READ `tests/test_native.py`, `test_vector_kernel.py`, `test_signals_kernel.py`, `test_matcher_parity.py`, `test_traverse.py`). Eleven oracle modules back these parity tests (EXECUTED, `ls tests/support/oracles`). The gram index's no-false-negative law (C9) has three fuzz tests named for it in `tests/models/test_code_grams.py` (READ).

What a pass proves is weaker than it reads. The seeds are fixed, so every run checks the same worlds: the suite never explores a world it has not already passed. A failure would not shrink to a minimal case. And the worlds are small by design: six directories, a handful of files, three users and a few groups (READ `tests/support/grant_worlds.py`). That small universe is a strength for one technique and a limit for this one. It is a limit because 400 samples is a thin slice of the space. It is a strength because the space is finite, so it could be searched exhaustively or nearly so (section 6). The chunker has no oracle: `tests/models/test_chunking.py` names none, so `chunk.rs` is pinned on examples only (EXECUTED, grep, INFERRED).

## 4. Pins by lint and by gate

The lints in `tests/lint/` check the shape of the tree and its documents, not what the code does (READ). For code they hold the import ladder and the adapter's isolation (C18), the module size baseline, the one home of the clock, and the rule that `src/` names no context document. For documents they hold dates, one-line paragraphs, link resolution, claim labels and the absence of open-question markers. One lint touches a README claim directly: `test_the_readme_tells_the_truth_after_the_yank` (READ). The host-access claim (C16) has no lint. A grep finds that `src/` imports `os` once, in `offload.py`, for `os.cpu_count()` and `os.register_at_fork`, and imports no `subprocess`, `socket`, `urllib`, `httpx` or `requests` and calls no bare `open(` (EXECUTED). So the claim is true today and held by nothing.

One claim is proven for every input by a closed-form argument: every lawful path fits every declared key budget (C17), because the worst case is `MAX_PATH_LENGTH` bytes and `test_every_lawful_path_fits_every_declared_key_budget` checks each profile's budget against that maximum (READ). Two more are proven over a finite vocabulary: the kind contract table is total over `ErrorKind` (`test_contract_table_is_total_over_the_vocabulary`), and the developer-only ops are absent from the agent-callable surface the drift test enumerates (C14, `tests/test_ops.py` asserts `frozenset(calls) == ALL_OPS - DEVELOPER_OPS` and that a recording router saw no developer op) (READ). C14 protects a surface that is not built yet: the README says the MCP surface is "drafted, not built" (READ), so the claim is about the future surface, and the pin guards the vocabulary that surface will be built from (INFERRED).

The coverage gate is `--cov-fail-under=100` in `scripts/ci.sh` and in the CI Tests job's 3.13 leg (READ). It measures line coverage only: `[tool.coverage.run]` sets no `branch = true` (READ `pyproject.toml`). Its exclusions include the pattern `\.\.\.`, which the placeholder memo above measures as excluding 111 whole functions, about 1,400 statements (READ). It measures Python only. The Rust crate is reached through the pyo3 seam by the Python tests, but no Rust line is measured (INFERRED from the absence of `cargo llvm-cov` anywhere, EXECUTED by grep).

The Rust crate's own tests (C8) are outside every gate. `crates/vfs-core/src` holds 117 `#[test]` functions in 23 test modules (EXECUTED, grep), and AGENTS.md says to keep them green, but neither `scripts/ci.sh` nor any workflow under `.github/workflows/` runs `cargo test`, `clippy` or `miri` (EXECUTED, grep for `cargo|clippy|llvm-cov|miri` finds only a comment in `ci.sh`). A Rust test that goes red is seen only by whoever runs it by hand. Run by hand here, in a scratch target directory, the crate is green today: `cargo test -p vfs-core` reported 115 passed in the unit tests and 2 in the doc tests, 0 failed (EXECUTED). The crate holds two `unsafe` uses, both in `python.rs`, the pyo3 binding (EXECUTED, grep).

The documentation gate (C19) covers `README.md` and `docs/{tutorials,how-to,reference,explanation}/*.md` through Sybil (READ `conftest.py`). The new `documentation/` folder is not in that list. Its tutorial tells the reader to call `fs.cli("ls")`; `hasattr(vfs.VirtualFileSystem, "cli")` and the async twin both print `False`, and no `def cli` exists under `src/vfs` (EXECUTED). The folder is untracked work in progress, so this is a gap in the gate's reach rather than a published error, but it is exactly the drift the Sybil gate exists to stop (INFERRED). The overview also says the kernel's second question is "Is the caller identified?", while the README's Known limits say a fresh storage's posture is `read_write` and "an anonymous caller may read and write on it" (READ): C15 as written is contradicted by the README, and no test pins the four-question order beyond the pairwise gate-order tests (`test_gate_order_capability_outranks_permission`, `test_gate_order_busy_outranks_capability_and_permission`) (READ).

The mutant ledger (C22) is vfs's mutation practice. It holds about 80 rows, each a one-line bug that once survived the suite and is now killed by a named pin (EXECUTED, 86 table lines including headers). It is replayed during review campaigns by the `test_review` skill, "not as standing CI" (READ). So a mutation score is earned by hand, per campaign, over the rows a review touches. The working-tree ledger's replay protocol says `git worktree add`, which AGENTS.md and `testing.md` both forbid (READ); the file is mid-edit in the working tree, so this study records the conflict without grading it.

## 5. Pins on real engines

The four engine legs run through `.github/workflows/test-dialects.yml` on a push to `main` and on pull requests, but only when the change touches `src/vfs/storage/**`, `src/vfs/models/**`, `tests/**`, `docker/compose.test.yml`, `pyproject.toml` or the workflow itself (READ). A change confined to the router (`src/vfs/base.py`), the permissions module, the results package or the Rust crate does not start them (INFERRED from the path list). Most changes also touch `tests/`, which does start them, so the gap is narrow, but a Rust-only edit to the vector kernel, the referee every dialect's ranking is pinned against, would land without the engines that ranking is compared on (READ `vector.py` docstring, INFERRED). Locally the legs are deselected unless `VFS_TEST_<ENGINE>_URL` is set: the default collection is 4,900 of 6,449 tests, with 1,549 engine-leg tests deselected (EXECUTED). The README's "4,564 tests" (C20) is stale against 6,449 collected; the "green on five engines" half is true as of the last workflow run that met the path filter, and this study did not run the engine legs.

## 6. Four experiments: what the cheapest raise of each pin finds

**04-a, the conformance contract on the generic floor (C6).** A ten-line pytest plugin, [generic_floor_plugin.py](experiments/04/generic_floor_plugin.py), swaps the engine host's profile resolver so SQLite resolves to `GENERIC` stamped `exotic`, as an unknown dialect would, and runs `TestSqliteConformance` (both contracts, 344 tests). EXECUTED: the control passed 344; the floor passed 307, skipped 36 and failed one, `test_move_batch_is_staged_atomic`. Keeping SQLite's PRAGMAs and retry codes did not change that. Taking one differing field at a time from the floor into SQLite's own profile, all sixteen policy fields passed the failing test; changing only the name to `exotic` failed it. The cause is in `engine.py`: pysqlite's transaction control is installed only `if profile.name == "sqlite"` (READ). With the name kept, the floor's policy passed 308 and skipped the 36 search tests, exactly the withholding AGENTS.md promises (EXECUTED). So the floor's policy does serve the core verbs on this engine. The experiment also shows what the claim leans on that no profile field states: that an unknown dialect's driver opens real transactions. vfs fixes pysqlite's by name; an unknown driver with the same defect would lose batch atomicity silently (INFERRED). Raising C6 to "tested on the contract" is one new class in `tests/storage/test_conformance.py`.

**04-b, bind width against batch width (C5).** [statement_width.py](experiments/04/statement_width.py) squeezes SQLite's parameter budget to 999 and runs write, stat, glob, reindex, grep, search and delete over 100, 1,000 and 5,000 files with a listener recording every statement's bind count. EXECUTED: the widest statement carried 982 binds at every width; the statement count grew with the batch (write: 39, 119, 469) while the widest statement did not. This is the claim's universal form for seven verbs on one engine, and it holds. As a standing pin it would be a fixture, not a script: the listener in `tests/conftest.py` (or `tests/support/statements.py`) attached to every engine the suite builds, asserting each statement's binds against `host.parameter_budget` and its `IN` elements against `profile.in_list_budget`, so every one of the 4,900 tests becomes a width check (INFERRED). Running the suite once more with the budget squeezed would make SQLite behave like the tightest engine for every call site at once (INFERRED).

**04-c, the `Result` laws as properties (C11).** [result_algebra_properties.py](experiments/04/result_algebra_properties.py) states associativity, identity, the success homomorphism and the wire round trip as Hypothesis properties over generated envelopes, 2,000 examples a law. EXECUTED: three laws held; identity failed in about nine seconds. The counterexample Hypothesis shrank to is an envelope holding two equal errors: `a` has 2 errors, `a | Result()` has 2, `Result() | a` has 1. The cause is `_combined_errors` in `envelope.py`, which keeps the left side's errors verbatim and drops repeats only from the right side, so the empty envelope on the left deduplicates and on the right does not (READ). The suite's identity test passes because its one example carries one error (READ). Whether a verb can produce two value-equal errors in one envelope was not settled in the box; the law, as the docstring states it, is false on a constructible value.

**04-d, the rights resolver under a wider search (C3).** [rights_resolver_search.py](experiments/04/rights_resolver_search.py) drives the suite's own world builder through Hypothesis's random source, so each run draws new worlds. EXECUTED: 20,000 worlds passed, 0 failed, in 76 seconds, against the suite's 400 from one seed. This raises the resolver from "the same 400 worlds" to "fresh worlds every run, shrinking on failure", and found nothing: the most important algebra in vfs held under fifty times the search (EXECUTED).

## 8. Ranked recommendations

1. **Gate `cargo test -p vfs-core` in `scripts/ci.sh` and `.github/workflows/test.yml`.** 117 tests that no gate runs are a claim with no pin, and the fix is one step in two files (EXECUTED evidence in section 4).
2. **Add Hypothesis to the dev group and convert the 33 fixed-seed tests, starting with `tests/storage/test_grants.py`, `tests/storage/database/test_rights.py` and `tests/results/test_result_laws.py`.** The world builders already exist; 04-d shows the conversion is a few lines, and 04-c shows it finds a broken law in seconds where the examples did not.

The one thing in this dimension that "100% certainty" most misleads about is the coverage figure. "100% coverage" reads as "every behaviour is checked". It means every non-excluded Python line ran at least once under some test. It says nothing about branches, nothing about the 1,400 excluded statements, nothing about the Rust engine, and nothing about whether any assertion would notice that line being wrong. The broken identity law in 04-c sits on fully covered lines.

## What this study does not settle

The box did not reach the real-engine legs: no Postgres, MariaDB, SQL Server or Oracle run was made, so the "green on five engines" half of C20 is graded from the workflow's text, not from a run. 04-c establishes that the identity law fails on a constructible envelope; whether any verb produces two value-equal errors in one envelope was not traced. 04-a ran the floor on SQLite only, which tests the floor's policy, not an unknown engine's behaviour. The cross-mount merge (C12), ownership by construction, the epoch coherence of grep and the renderer limits were graded from test names, not read in depth. Of the 30 numbered specs under `context/specifications/active/`, only four status lines were read; their claims are not in the table unless the README or a docstring repeats them.
