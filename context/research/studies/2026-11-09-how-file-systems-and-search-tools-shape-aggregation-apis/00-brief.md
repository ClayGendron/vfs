## The brief every researcher received

You are researching for the repository at `/Users/claygendron/Git/Repos/vfs`, a Python library that gives AI agents one governed namespace over mounted storage backends, with a required Rust engine. Its verbs are Unix-shaped: `ls`, `tree`, `stat`, `read`, `glob`, `grep`, `glean` (ranked search), `graph` (typed edges), `write`, `edit`, `delete`, and more, each returning one `Result` whose rows are `Observation` records with optional fields (`kind`, `ext`, `mime_type`, `size_bytes`, `version`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `in_degree`, `out_degree`, `depth`, `score`, `matches`). A caller asks for the fields it wants through a `columns` parameter, and the renderer prints only those. Today the only aggregation in vfs is a summary line the renderer prints under a listing, counting files and directories (`src/vfs/results/render.py`, the `_file_summary` helper). `read` takes no range. Nothing counts by attribute. Nothing rolls a measure up a subtree. The design under consideration has three layers, cheapest first: a typed stats block on every listing and search result (entries matched, entries returned, bytes, a breakdown by kind, and for search the total before truncation and whether it is exact); rollup columns on directory observations (`file_count`, `dir_count`, `total_bytes`, `total_lines` over the subtree, fetched only when asked, so `tree(max_depth=1, columns={"total_bytes"})` is `du -d 1`); and a histogram verb that takes a scope root, an attribute to group by and a top-N, and returns buckets with counts, an "other" bucket, the total and a truncated flag. You may read the vfs tree, read-only: `src/vfs/base.py` for the verb signatures, `src/vfs/models/entry.py` for `Observation`, `src/vfs/results/render.py` for the summary line, `AGENTS.md` for the production posture (every statement bounded by a declared budget, the least generous SQL engine as the floor, counts judged against the caller's rights so a number never leaks a file the caller may not list). The design is not yours to make; your job is to report how the projects named in your question made the same choices, so the spec that follows stands on evidence.

Your deliverable answers, for each project in your question: where the aggregation lives in the API (a verb of its own, an option on a listing or search verb, a column the caller asks for, or a side channel on every result); what it measures (count, bytes, lines, distinct values) and over what unit (a file, a subtree, a result set, a bucket); how the caller chooses the grain (a depth, a group-by key, a bucket width, a top-N); how the output is shaped (a text footer, a structured record, a nested tree of buckets) and whether structured output exists for programs to consume; how truncation is reported (a cap, a truncated flag, an "other" bucket, exact versus lower-bound totals); whether totals are computed on demand or maintained incrementally (cached subtree sizes, per-directory counters, index-time facet fields) and what invalidates them; and how, if at all, the aggregation respects the caller's rights or visibility. ## The questions

Researcher 01, rollup verbs in coreutils and disk-usage tools. Study `du` and `wc` in `uutils-coreutils` (`src/uu/du/src/du.rs`, `src/uu/wc/src/wc.rs`: depth, summarize, total, threshold, apparent size, the output format, how the rollup walks and what it carries per directory); GNU `tree` in the `tree` clone (`--du`, `-s`, `-h`, `--filelimit`, the footer line, the `-J` JSON and `-X` XML outputs and how the report element carries counts); `dust` (its tree of nodes with sizes, depth and count limits, how it chooses what to show); `ncdu` (its scan model, the export format it writes and reads, and anything it caches); `macdirstat` (the subtree size cache and what invalidates it); the `du` of `plan9port`, `toybox`, `busybox` and `sbase` for the smallest shape the verb has had; and the structured shells `nushell` (`du`, `ls` with sizes, `histogram`, `group-by`, `uniq -c`: how aggregation becomes a table rather than text) and `PowerShell` (`Get-ChildItem` piped to `Measure-Object` and `Group-Object`: aggregation as a verb over records). Experiment: run the system `du -d 1` and `wc` over the vfs `src/` and `context/` directories, and, if the `tree` clone builds within three minutes, `tree --du -J` over `src/vfs`, and show how each reports totals, depth and truncation. Write to `01-rollup-verbs-in-coreutils-and-disk-usage-tools.md`, experiments under `experiments/01-rollup-verbs-in-coreutils-and-disk-usage-tools/`.

Researcher 02, stats and count modes on search and code tools. Study `ripgrep` (`--stats`, `-c`, `--count-matches`, `-l`, `--files-with-matches`, `--files`, and the `summary` message of `--json`: what each counts, how a count interacts with `--max-count`, where the stats struct lives and what fills it); `zoekt` (the `Stats` struct in `api.go`, which searcher fields fill it, `SearchOptions` limits such as `MaxDocDisplayCount` and `TotalMaxMatchCount`, how a truncated result still reports what was skipped, and the `zoekt list` and repository-stats surfaces); `codesearch` (what its index and search report); `git` (`grep -c`, `grep -l`, `ls-files` with `wc`, `shortlog -sn`, `diff --stat`, `--numstat`, `--shortstat`, `count-objects`, `rev-list --count`: aggregation as flags on verbs that otherwise list); `tokei` (the library API: `Languages`, `Language`, `Report`, `Stats`; the `--output json` shape; `--sort`, `--files`, `--exclude`; how it rolls per-file stats up to per-language totals and how blanks, comments and code are counted); and `scc` (`--format`, `--by-file`, `-s`, the COCOMO estimate, how it reports totals). Experiment: run `rg --stats`, `rg -c` and `rg --json` for one pattern over `src/`, and `git shortlog -sn` and `git diff --shortstat HEAD~5` in the vfs checkout; if `tokei` or `scc` builds within three minutes, run it over `src/` with JSON output. Write to `02-stats-and-count-modes-on-search-and-code-tools.md`, experiments under `experiments/02-stats-and-count-modes-on-search-and-code-tools/`.

Researcher 03, facets and aggregations in query engines. Study `datasette` (`datasette/facets.py`: the `Facet` base class, `ColumnFacet`, `ArrayFacet`, `DateFacet`, the `suggest` and `facet_results` methods, how `facet_size`, `facet_time_limit_ms` and `facet_suggest_time_limit_ms` in `app.py` bound the work, the `truncated` flag and the JSON shape of a facet result); `sqlite-utils` (`analyze-tables` and `--common-limit`: per-column counts, most and least common values, how it bounds the scan); the `lucene` facet module (`lucene/facet`: `FacetsConfig`, `Facets`, `FacetResult`, `LabelAndValue`, `getTopChildren`, `getAllDims`, `SortedSetDocValuesFacetCounts` versus the taxonomy index, drill-down and drill-sideways, and how counts are stored at index time rather than computed by scanning); the `tantivy` aggregation module (`src/aggregation`: the request and response types, `terms`, `histogram`, `date_histogram`, `range`, `stats`, nesting of sub-aggregations, `size`, `sum_other_doc_count`, `doc_count_error_upper_bound`, and the memory limit in `AggregationLimits`); and `qdrant` (the facet endpoint: key, limit, filter, the `exact` flag and what an inexact count means). Experiment: make a small SQLite table under your experiments folder from a listing of the vfs `src/` tree (path, ext, size, lines) and run `sqlite-utils analyze-tables` and a datasette facet query against it, through `uv run` with a throwaway environment if neither is installed and the install takes under three minutes; record the JSON. Write to `03-facets-and-aggregations-in-query-engines.md`, experiments under `experiments/03-facets-and-aggregations-in-query-engines/`.

Researcher 04, aggregation in file-system libraries and filesystem-over-database peers. Study `filesystem_spec` (fsspec: `du`, `disk_usage`, `find` with `withdirs`, `maxdepth` and `detail`, `info`, `ls` with `detail`, `sizes`; how `du` sums and whether any backend caches); `pyfilesystem2` (`glob` and its `count` method returning a `Counts` record of files, directories and data; `Info` and its namespaces; `walk` and `Step`; `getsize`); `opendal` (`list` with `recursive`, `Metadata`, `stat`, any usage or stats surface in the core or the bindings); `juicefs` (directory statistics: how the metadata engine maintains per-directory usage and file counts incrementally, the `dir-stats` feature, `juicefs info` and `quota`, and what a write does to the counters); `seaweedfs` (the filer's directory and collection statistics, `fs.du` in `weed shell`); `minio` (`ListObjectsV2` with a delimiter: `CommonPrefixes`, `KeyCount`, `MaxKeys`, `IsTruncated` as the S3 shape of rolling up by prefix, and minio's own data-usage crawler and `DataUsageInfo`); and the agent-facing peers `agentfs`, `mirage`, `afs` (its `explain` verb), `computer` (the `dofs` inode tree) and `eve`, reporting whether any of them aggregates at all and how. Also read POSIX `statvfs` through the `freebsd-src` or `linux` clone, briefly, for the smallest aggregate a file system has always offered. Experiment: with `uv run --with fsspec python -I` on a script under your experiments folder, call fsspec's local `du` with and without `total` and `maxdepth` over the vfs `src/` directory, and `pyfilesystem2`'s `glob("**/*.py").count()` if it installs within three minutes; record the records returned. Write to `04-aggregation-in-file-system-libraries-and-database-peers.md`, experiments under `experiments/04-aggregation-in-file-system-libraries-and-database-peers/`.
