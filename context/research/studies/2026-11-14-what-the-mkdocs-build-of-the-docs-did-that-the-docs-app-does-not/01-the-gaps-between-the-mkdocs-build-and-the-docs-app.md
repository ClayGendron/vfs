# The docs app keeps the pages, the navigation and the look of the MkDocs build, and drops five things a reader would notice and eight smaller ones: the labels on API names, the table of contents on any window narrower than 1280 pixels, the link mark beside a heading, the new tab for a link that leaves the site, and the page furniture a search engine, a printer and a keyboard rely on

I was asked, as the one researcher of [the brief](00-brief.md) on 2026-11-14, to go through every feature and behaviour the MkDocs build of `documentation/` had, from its own sources and from the built site, and to say for each one what the docs app in `docs-app/` does. The time box was 40 minutes. I used about 17 minutes of it, from 17:10 to 17:27 EST by the machine's clock. Both sites were built in a scratch folder and compared as built, in Chromium, at four window widths, with scripts on and off, and in print. The count is 5 High gaps, 8 Medium gaps and 14 Low ones, and 36 items where the new site does the same, does more, or differs with no loss. Three things were skipped and are named in the last section: browsers other than Chromium, a real screen reader, and a side-by-side check of fine styling such as table row hover.

## Three facts about the two builds that the comparison rests on

The old site was rebuilt from today's pages, so it holds more API pages than its final form published. EXECUTED: the hook `overrides/api_pages.py` generated five pages under `reference/api/` (`AsyncVirtualFileSystem`, `Entry`, `MountError`, `MountInfo`, `Result`), and the twelve hand-written pages under `documentation/reference/python-api/`, which use the same `::: vfs.Name` line, were rendered by mkdocstrings too and reported as left out of the nav. The copy of the docs app was taken while the author was restoring the labels. READ: in the copy, `src/lib/docs/ApiBlock.svelte` already draws `<span data-api-kind>` in a class's heading, and draws nothing on a member or in the table of contents. EXECUTED: the export I ran at about 17:11 had no `labels` key. READ: by 17:16 the working tree's `scripts/docs_export.py` described a `labels` field holding `async`, `property` and `classmethod`. So gaps 1 and 2 below describe a moving target, and the counts I give for the old site are what a finished restoration can be checked against.

The MkDocs build of `documentation/` was a preview and never the published site. READ: line 1 of `mkdocs-documentation.yml` says "mkdocs.yml still builds the published site from docs/". INFERRED: no outside link points at an address or an anchor of the old build, so a changed anchor or a missing trailing slash breaks nothing that exists.

## The High gaps

### 1. The kind label before an API name

What is missing: the small word that says what a name is. EXECUTED: the old pages drew 340 of them in headings and one on every row of the table of contents, in five kinds, and the drawn text is `meth`, `attr`, `class`, `func` and `mod`. The author is restoring this, so the only thing this study adds is the check: [api-anatomy.txt](experiments/01-the-gaps-between-the-mkdocs-build-and-the-docs-app/api-anatomy.txt) holds the old counts, and the restoration is complete when a member's heading and its table-of-contents row carry the label, not the class's heading alone. The table of contents needs its own change: READ, `TocEntry` in `types.ts` has an id, a text and a depth and no kind, so `TableOfContents.svelte` has nothing to draw one from.

### 2. The declaration label after an API name

What is missing: the word that says how a name is declared. The one a reader needs is `async`. EXECUTED: the old `AsyncVirtualFileSystem` page marked 33 methods `async`, and the new page says `async` once in all its text, so nothing on a method's entry tells a reader to `await` it. The same loss, smaller: 32 names were marked `property`, so a reader knew not to call them, and 9 were marked `classmethod`. The old screenshot is [old-api-bind-1280.png](experiments/01-the-gaps-between-the-mkdocs-build-and-the-docs-app/browser/old-api-bind-1280.png) and the new one is [new-api-bind-1280.png](experiments/01-the-gaps-between-the-mkdocs-build-and-the-docs-app/browser/new-api-bind-1280.png).

What closing it takes. READ: the working tree's `scripts/docs_export.py` was gaining a `labels` field as I worked, which is the first half. The second half is in the app: `labels` on `ApiObject` and `ApiMember` in `types.ts`, passed through by `apiObject()` in `markdown.server.ts`, and drawn after the name in `ApiBlock.svelte`, in the same small mono text the class label already uses. INFERRED: `async` could also be said in the signature itself, since `declaration()` already highlights a signature as a definition by putting `def ` in front and cutting it off again. INFERRED: `instance-attribute` and `class-attribute` are the two a reader needs least, and 105 of the 242 old labels were those two, so restoring `async`, `property`, `classmethod` and `dataclass` alone closes what matters.

### 3. The table of contents on a window narrower than 1280 pixels

What is missing: on a laptop window of 1100 pixels, on a tablet and on a phone, a page has no list of its headings at all. EXECUTED: the old site showed the list as a column at 1100 pixels and inside the drawer at 390; the new site shows it at 1280 pixels and at none of 390, 800 and 1100. This is worst on the API pages, where the list is the only index of a class's members: `AsyncVirtualFileSystem` has 39. The two drawers are [old-drawer-390.png](experiments/01-the-gaps-between-the-mkdocs-build-and-the-docs-app/browser/old-drawer-390.png) and [new-drawer-390.png](experiments/01-the-gaps-between-the-mkdocs-build-and-the-docs-app/browser/new-drawer-390.png).

What closing it takes. Two places, and either alone helps. First, the drawer: in `src/routes/+layout.svelte`, under the current page's row, list `page.data.page.toc` with the installed sidebar item's `Sidebar.MenuSub`, which is how the old drawer did it. Second, the middle widths: in `src/routes/[...route]/+page.svelte`, above the article, a closed "table of contents" block drawn with the installed `collapsible` item, shown where `TableOfContents.svelte` is hidden. INFERRED: lowering the column's breakpoint alone is not enough, because at 1024 pixels two 256 pixel panels leave the article 512.

### 4. The link mark beside a heading

What is missing: a way to copy the address of a section. EXECUTED: every old heading held a link to itself, shown on hover, 14 on Overview and one on each API member; a new heading holds none. A reader can still get a section's address by clicking its row in the table of contents, but only where gap 3 leaves that list visible. For an API reference this is the common act of sending someone the link to one method.

What closing it takes. In `src/lib/docs/Markdown.svelte`, the heading branch draws an anchor to `#{block.id}` inside the heading, and `ApiBlock.svelte` does the same for its two heading levels. Its look goes in `src/lib/docs/prose.css`: hidden until the heading is hovered or the link is focused, so a keyboard reaches it. The ids exist already, and the prerender already fails on an anchor that leads nowhere.

### 5. A link that leaves the site

What is missing: three things the old build did by hand in three files. A link to another site opened in a new tab, so the docs stayed open. A screen reader was told "Opens in a new tab" through `aria-describedby`. The header's GitHub link carried a small arrow that said it leaves the site. EXECUTED: on the new site the 8 outside links on 2 pages and the header link have no `target` and no `rel`, and open in the same tab. READ: the spec's text does not hold the words "new tab", so this was not left out on purpose.

What closing it takes. `routeOf()` in `markdown.server.ts` already tells a link with a scheme from a link inside the docs, so it can mark the link as external on the `link` kind in `types.ts`. `Inline.svelte` then adds `target="_blank"`, `rel="noopener"` and `aria-describedby`, and `+layout.svelte` holds the one hidden sentence the attribute names, and gives its own GitHub link the same three attributes and an arrow from the installed `phosphor-svelte` icons.

## The Medium gaps

### 6. Back to top

EXECUTED: the old site showed a "Back to top" button once the reader scrolled up a little; the new site has no control of the kind. It matters on the long pages, and the API pages are the long ones. Closing it: a `Button` from the installed `button` item, drawn by `src/routes/[...route]/+page.svelte` or the layout, shown after the first screen has scrolled away. INFERRED: a plain "back to top" link at the foot of `TableOfContents.svelte` and beside the pager is the smaller version, with no script.

### 7. Skip to content

EXECUTED: the old site's first Tab stop was "Skip to content", so one Tab and Enter reached the article; the new site's first stop is the wordmark, and the article is 12 Tab presses away on Overview and 23 on the `AsyncVirtualFileSystem` page. A reader who uses a keyboard and no mouse walks the header and the whole left navigation on every page. The new site is not worse in raw distance: the old site without its skip link was 28 and 57 presses on the same two pages. What is lost is the shortcut. Closing it: one link as the first element of `src/routes/+layout.svelte`, hidden until focused, that points at an `id` on the `<main>` element.

### 8. The page's description and canonical address

EXECUTED: every old page carried a description, a canonical address (the one address a search engine should index for the page) and previous and next links in its head; one new page of 26 has a description and none has the other two. A reader meets this as a search result or a pasted link with no summary under it. Closing it: `src/routes/[...route]/+page.svelte` already writes the title in `<svelte:head>` and can write the rest. A description better than the old one, which repeated one sentence on every page, is the page's first paragraph: `renderPage()` in `markdown.server.ts` has it as a block and can return its plain text on `Page` in `types.ts`. The canonical address needs the site's origin, which `src/lib/docs/site.ts` does not hold yet; decision 1 names it as `docs.vfs.dev`.

### 9. The sitemap

EXECUTED: the old build wrote `sitemap.xml` with 31 addresses; the new build writes none, and no `robots.txt`. No reader sees this, and it matters only once the site is served. Closing it: a prerendered route, `src/routes/sitemap.xml/+server.ts`, that lists `routes()` from `content.server.ts` under the same origin as gap 8.

### 10. The icon in the browser tab

EXECUTED: the new pages link no icon and `/favicon.ico` answers 404, so the tab shows the browser's blank page. The old icon was Material's own mark, so what is lost is an icon, not a VFS one. Closing it: a `static/` folder in `docs-app/`, which does not exist yet, holding the icon, and one `<link rel="icon">` in `src/app.html`. VFS has no logo, so the icon itself is a design choice for the owner.

### 11. Printing

EXECUTED: printed, the old Overview is the article at the page's full width; the new one prints the header and the left navigation on its first page and keeps that column empty on every page after, so the text runs in about three fifths of the width. The two are [old-overview-print.pdf](experiments/01-the-gaps-between-the-mkdocs-build-and-the-docs-app/browser/old-overview-print.pdf) and [new-overview-print.pdf](experiments/01-the-gaps-between-the-mkdocs-build-and-the-docs-app/browser/new-overview-print.pdf). Closing it: one `@media print` block in `src/lib/docs/site.css` that hides `[data-slot="docs-header"]`, the sidebar, `[data-slot="docs-toc"]`, `[data-slot="docs-pager"]` and the copy buttons, and lets `[data-slot="docs-article"]` take the width.

### 12. What a module's page lists

EXECUTED: on the Permissions page the old build drew `Permission = Literal['read', 'read_write']` with its docstring, and the three fields of `PermissionLayer`; the new page has neither, and the definition of `Permission` is on no page of the new site, though signatures on other pages name it. The old build also turned the three headings inside the module's docstring into anchors, where the new one draws bold lines, which the renderer does on purpose. Closing it is in `scripts/docs_export.py`, whose member list for a module is "a module's own functions and classes": add the module's documented attributes, with annotation and value, and the members of each class it lists. `ApiBlock.svelte` then needs one more heading level for a class inside a module.

### 13. With scripts turned off

EXECUTED: with scripts off the new pages still show their text, their diagrams and their highlighted code, since every page is written as HTML at build time. Two things fail. A reader whose system is dark gets the light theme, because the theme is a `dark` class a script sets, where the old stylesheet followed the system by itself. On a phone the drawer does not open, so the navigation is the pager alone. Closing the first is a change to the design system's `theme` item, a rule that follows `prefers-color-scheme` when no class has been set; decision 3 says an item is not edited in this repository. Closing the second is a `<noscript>` list of the tabs in `src/routes/+layout.svelte`. INFERRED: few readers meet either.

## What stayed unsettled, and what would settle each

- Whether the author's restoration covers gap 2 as well as gap 1. I saw the export gaining `labels` and did not see the app draw them. Rebuilding after that change and counting the labels against the 242 in `api-anatomy.txt` settles it.
- Whether the fine styling matches: the row hover on a table, a column's width, a name in code that never breaks inside a table cell, the group label that stays put in the left navigation. `theme.css` was read by its comments and both sites were photographed, but the two were not laid side by side rule by rule. Screenshots of Access Levels at 390, 800 and 1280 pixels on both sites would settle it.
- Whether any gap differs in Safari or Firefox. Every browser run was Chromium. The inline diagram exists because of a Safari behaviour, so Safari is the one worth a run.
- What a screen reader says. The `aria` attributes were read from the built HTML and no screen reader was run. VoiceOver on one outside link and on the drawer would settle gaps 5 and 7.
- How the host answers an old-style address such as `/overview/`, and whether it serves `404.html`. My loopback server mapped both by hand. This belongs to the deployment spec and is settled by the first deployment.
- Why the old page's signature block for `bind` is blank in one saved screenshot. The signature's text is in the built HTML, so no finding rests on the picture; scrolling the old page by hand would show whether it was the screenshot's timing.

## One line

The docs app draws the same pages with the same look and loses the reader's small tools: restore the labels on API names (kind, and above all `async`), give narrow windows and phones a table of contents, put a link mark on headings, open outside links in a new tab, and then add the back-to-top, skip link, head metadata, sitemap, icon and print rules.
