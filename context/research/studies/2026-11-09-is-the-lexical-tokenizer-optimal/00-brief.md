## The question (from Clay, 2026-11-09)

The lexical tokenizer in `crates/vfs-core/src/lexical.rs` (`tokenize`, with `fold_into`, `emit`, `identifier_parts`, `case_boundary`, the `is_*` class checks over the generated tables in `lexical_tables.rs`) turns a body into the BM25 index's terms and glean's query terms. [Study 03 of the dirty-row budgets memo](../2026-11-09-the-dirty-row-budgets-of-grep-and-glean/03-the-overlay-counts-kernel-spike.md) measured it in pure Rust at 64 µs per 7,167-byte body, 112 MB/s and 59 ns a token, and found about 22 µs of that is the per-term `String` clone in `emit` and about 17 µs more is the `Vec<char>` collection plus the fold buffer, since a byte-level probe of the same rules on all-ASCII bodies ran at 24 µs. Clay asked that the tokenizer be researched "to make sure it is optimal". The multiplier that makes this matter is the index build: AGENTS.md's reference deployment has about 11,000,000 index visits a day, and the build tokenizes every chunk of every visited file; [the 2026-10-31 baseline study](../2026-10-31-incremental-index-refresh/02-baseline-measurements.md) measured the lexical build at 64 s of a 136 s rebuild of 50,000 files.

## What is fixed, and what is free

Fixed: the tokenizer's rules and its output. Word runs are maximal runs of word characters (letters, digits, underscore) by the generated CPython 3.13.11 / Unicode 15.1 tables; a run is emitted whole and, when it has more than one part, its parts, split on `_` and on case change with digit-led pieces whole; terms are case-folded with the Turkic-i pre-fold and the interpreter's casefold map; terms under 2 characters or over 64 bytes after folding are dropped; duplicates are kept in order. `TOKENIZER_VERSION = 1` in `src/vfs/models/lexical.py` enters the index's options fingerprint, and the pure-Python oracle `tests/support/oracles/lexical.py::pure_tokenize` referees the Rust through `tests/models/test_lexical.py`. A change that alters one emitted term for any input is a tokenizer version bump and a rebuild of every index, so it is out of this study's scope: optimal here means the same output faster, or a documented reason the current speed is already right.

Free: everything about how the output is produced. The `Vec<char>` collection, the per-term `String` clone, the per-character table lookups (ASCII fast path today, binary search over range tables otherwise), the fold into a scratch buffer, the `chars().count()` length check in `emit`, the identifier-part split, the builder's use of the terms in `LexicalAccumulator` (how it counts and keys them), and the shape of the Python-facing binding (`python.rs` `tokenize` returning `Vec<String>` as a Python list).

## The two studies

01, prior art (reads clones, runs nothing heavy): how three to five mature tokenizers of the same job produce terms, and what they measure. Candidates among the catalogued clones under `~/Git/Repos/`: `tantivy` (its `SimpleTokenizer`, `LowerCaser`, `SplitCompoundWords` and the `TokenStream` trait that avoids allocation per token), `lucene` (`StandardTokenizer`'s JFlex scanner and `CharTermAttribute` buffer reuse, `WordDelimiterGraphFilter` for the identifier split), `bm25s` (Python, regex-based, what it measures), `zoekt` (trigram, not a word tokenizer; note only how it scans bytes), `pg_textsearch` (its parser). Per the rules in `context/research/README.md` and AGENTS.md "Reference repos for research": refresh each clone to its upstream default branch first, read and log the licence, record the commit, never copy code, describe and cite. Report, per project: how it represents a token (owned string, borrowed slice, offsets into the text, reused buffer), how it classifies characters (table, `char` methods, byte-level ASCII path), how it folds case, whether and how it splits identifiers, whether it works on bytes or decoded characters, and any throughput figure it publishes or benchmarks it ships. End with what our tokenizer does differently from the consensus and why that might cost time.

02, measurement (pure Rust, this machine): a scratch crate depending on `vfs-core` by path (or including `lexical.rs` and `lexical_tables.rs` by `#[path]`, which is our own code), over 2,000 bodies from study 02's generator (`../2026-11-09-the-dirty-row-budgets-of-grep-and-glean/experiments/corpus.py`, `generate(2000)`) plus a second corpus of real text with non-ASCII, mixed-case identifiers and underscores (the repository's own `src/` and `crates/` files and the `context/` prose are enough, read from disk). First, profile where the 64 µs goes: time the stages separately (chars collection, run scanning and class checks, identifier split, fold, length check, clone) by variants that remove one stage at a time, and report µs per body and the share of each. Then measure candidate implementations that keep the output byte-identical, asserting equality with `vfs_core::tokenize` on every body before timing: (a) no `Vec<char>`: scan `char_indices` over the `&str` and work on byte-offset slices; (b) emit through a closure with a borrowed `&str`, no clone, as study 03's kernel does; (c) an ASCII fast path for a whole run that is ASCII, falling back to the general path otherwise, inside one function, not a second tokenizer; (d) `emit`'s length check as a byte count with the character count only when the bytes exceed the cap or are under the floor; (e) class checks through a 128-entry ASCII table plus the range search; (f) any combination the profile justifies. Then measure the index build's use: `LexicalAccumulator` (in `lexical.rs`, the builder) fed the same bodies as it is today against a variant that looks each term up as a borrowed slice and clones only when the term is new to the map, reporting µs per body and allocations per body (count them with a counting global allocator in the bench). Finally, run `reindex()` on study 02's 100,000-file SQLite store copy (under the scratchpad `scanbudgets/curve/`, clean base; copy it first) with the current engine and report the wall and the share the lexical build takes, so the memo can say what a faster tokenizer would save of the rebuild; do not rebuild the engine with a changed tokenizer, since the repo is read-only, but say what the bench figures project.
