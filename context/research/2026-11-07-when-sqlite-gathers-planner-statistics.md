# SQLite needs fresh, full statistics before a scoped glob plans well, so vfs should run PRAGMA optimize's own growth check after every committed SQLite mutation, with no row cap. Reindex alone is not enough: with no statistics, with capped statistics, or with statistics from a tiny store, the planner seeks a low-cardinality index and the glob runs 25 to 2,500 times slower

On 2026-11-07 the drafter of [the two-channel scope spec](../specifications/active/2026-11-07-storage-takes-scope-roots-and-arms-as-two-channels.md) measured that a 50,000-file SQLite store with no planner statistics seeks the tombstone index for every OR arm of a scoped glob. One fan took 77 s against 0.96 s after `ANALYZE`. That spec left the defect to its own spec and asked when vfs should gather SQLite planner statistics, and how. The developer's idea is to do it inside index maintenance (`reindex`). This memo weighs that idea against the alternatives and recommends one. One researcher worked for about two and a half hours. It read SQLite's source and documentation, vfs's engine, dialect, schema and reindex code, the four server engines' documentation, and six SQLite-using reference clones. It ran eleven experiments through `uv run python` against stores of 1,000 to 1,036,001 entry rows, written through `DatabaseStorage` or copied row for row from one. The scripts and their raw outputs live outside the repository, in the session scratch directory `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-claygendron-Git-Repos-vfs/d11bcc98-d265-4b56-89cb-1af47d18fa3d/scratchpad/research-sqlite-analyze/` (`build.py`, `capture.py`, `exp_verbs.py`, `exp_cost.py`, `exp_growth.py`, `exp_tiny.py`, `exp_seed.py`, `exp_memory.py`, `exp_xconn.py`, and a `.txt` beside each). One step was blocked and skipped: a run of `reindex` on a 56,000-row store, to plan the gram and lexical tables with and without statistics. A concurrent session's uncommitted edits broke `import vfs` in the working tree (a missing `filter_candidates`), and its rebuilt native extension speaks protocol 13 against HEAD's 12, so a HEAD snapshot could not run either. This memo informs a spec still to be written. It decides nothing.

Every clock below is EXECUTED on one Apple M1 Pro (macOS, Darwin 25.5.0) under Python 3.13.11, SQLite 3.50.4, aiosqlite 0.22.1 and SQLAlchemy 2.0.52. ## The answer

The trap is real and grows with the store. EXECUTED: the drafter's composed fan of 2,000 roots × 8 arms took 79.9 s with no statistics and 1.04 s after `ANALYZE`. A fan of 50 roots × 8 arms took 434 ms against 17.6 ms at 11,201 rows, 4.3 s against 18 ms at 103,601 rows, and 46.3 s against 17.8 ms at 1,036,001 rows. The cause is SQLite's defaults. READ: with no statistics, every index equality is assumed to match 10 rows, and a range on the path index is assumed to match 1/64 of the table. So `deleted_at IS NULL`, true of every live row, looks like the most selective arm. Two obvious fixes make things worse. EXECUTED: one `ANALYZE` at provisioning records the single root row, and the planner then scans the whole table for every glob, `ls` and `stat` until the next `ANALYZE`. The documented default `PRAGMA optimize` caps its sample at 2,000 rows per index, and at about a million rows that capped sample sends the fan back to the tombstone index (45 s). Full statistics hold the good plan even when 9× stale. Statistics taken at 1,000 rows and grown 100× do not. A post-commit `PRAGMA optimize=0x10002` (check every table, re-analyze one whose size moved about tenfold, no cap) solves all of these at once. EXECUTED: grown from 1 to 1,036,001 rows in 10,000-row commits, it re-analyzed the entries table three times, at 10,000, 70,000 and 450,000 rows. All three together cost 0.30 s, every other commit cost under 0.09 ms, and the final plans were the good ones. INFERRED, the recommendation: own the step in the engine host's shared mutation runner (`_mutate`). Declare it as a SQLite-only `DialectProfile` statement, because the four server engines maintain their own statistics. Run it after every committed mutation, outside the transaction, as best effort. Never run it at provisioning, and never cap it. Reindex then gets the step for free, because every reindex phase commits through the same runner. Reindex alone would leave every glob-only, never-reindexed store on the slow plan, and `SupportsReindex` declares reindex "never required for correctness".

## 1. How SQLite's statistics work

1.1 READ. `ANALYZE` writes one `sqlite_stat1` row per index: the index's row count, then for each key prefix the average number of rows sharing one value (`../sqlite/src/analyze.c:41-60`). An empty table gets no row (`analyze.c:1269`). `sqlite_stat4` holds samples only when SQLite is compiled with `SQLITE_ENABLE_STAT4`, and only under an unlimited analysis (`analyze.c:1285`). The documentation says the statistics are not updated as the data changes. They are read into memory when the schema is read (https://sqlite.org/lang_analyze.html).

1.2 EXECUTED. No interpreter vfs runs on has STAT4. `PRAGMA compile_options` lists only `ENABLE_DBSTAT_VTAB` for uv's CPython 3.11.14, 3.12.12 and 3.13.11 (SQLite 3.50.4) and 3.14.10 (SQLite 3.53.1). aiosqlite drives the stdlib `sqlite3` module (`aiosqlite.core.sqlite3 is sqlite3` is true), so `sqlite_stat1` is the only statistic vfs's SQLite ever has.

1.3 READ. With no statistics, a table is assumed to hold 1,048,576 rows (`../sqlite/src/build.c:1333`). Every index's first-column equality is assumed to match 10 rows, and a unique index 1 (`build.c:4585-4620`). The planner assumes a range with both bounds matches 1/64 of the index (`../sqlite/src/where.c:2231-2238`). A prefix `LIKE` on `path` under `case_sensitive_like` is such a range. INFERRED: so with no statistics, any equality on any indexed column beats the path range. The tombstone term `deleted_at IS NULL` rides inside every glob arm (`src/vfs/storage/backends/database/reads.py:500-538`, `descent.py:215-236`), and `ix_vfs_entries_deleted_at` exists (`src/vfs/models/rows.py:493`). That is the trap. The same holds for `kind` (`rows.py:433`), `ext` and the other low-cardinality indexes.

1.4 READ. `PRAGMA analysis_limit=N` stops `ANALYZE` after about N rows per index (https://sqlite.org/pragma.html). The source shows what the cap does to a low-cardinality column. With the cap in force, the row count is estimated, but each per-value average is computed from the rows actually scanned (`analyze.c:866-872`). EXECUTED: a column holding one value in all 1,036,001 rows is recorded as `1036001 2001` under a 2,000-row cap, `1036001 1001` under 1,000, and `1036001 1036001` with no cap. INFERRED from 1.3: a capped column then looks to match about N_cap rows, against the path range's estimate of rows/64. Once the table holds more than about 64 × the cap, the low-cardinality index wins again. That threshold is 128,000 rows for the 2,000 cap.

1.5 READ. `PRAGMA optimize`'s rules are in `../sqlite/src/pragma.c:2445-2514`. Its implementation is `pragma.c:2517-2640`. The default mask "is and always shall be 0x0fffe" (`pragma.c:2479`), and today it covers bit 0x2 (run `ANALYZE` where it might help) and bit 0x10 (cap every `ANALYZE` at the smaller of `analysis_limit` and `SQLITE_DEFAULT_OPTIMIZE_LIMIT`, 2,000, `pragma.c:33-59` and `2538-2546`). Bit 0x10000 checks every table even if this connection never queried it. Bit 0x1 only lists the `ANALYZE` statements it would run. A table qualifies when it is an ordinary table not named `sqlite_*`. It also needs one of three things: the 0x10000 bit is set, an index lacks a `sqlite_stat1` row, or the planner used its statistics on this connection (`TF_MaybeReanalyze`, set at `where.c:4300` and `6636`). Then it is analyzed when an index lacks statistics and the table is not empty, or when its size has moved about tenfold since the last analysis (`iRange = 33`, `pragma.c:2600`). Above 100 b-trees the cap shrinks further (`pragma.c:2631`). INFERRED: `0x10002` is the documented on-open mask, and it does not include 0x10. It therefore runs an uncapped `ANALYZE`, unless the connection sets `analysis_limit`. EXECUTED: it recorded `103601 103601` where the default mask and `0x10012` recorded `103601 2001`.

1.6 READ. The advice on https://sqlite.org/pragma.html and https://sqlite.org/lang_analyze.html: a short-lived connection runs `PRAGMA optimize` just before it closes. A long-lived one runs `PRAGMA optimize=0x10002` when it opens and plain `PRAGMA optimize` periodically ("perhaps once per day or once per hour"). Every application runs it after a schema change. From 3.46.0, `PRAGMA optimize` is preferred to a bare `ANALYZE` because it sets a temporary limit itself, and the source dates that limit's introduction to 2024-02-19 (`pragma.c:2470`). vfs declares no minimum SQLite version (READ: no `sqlite_version` check in `src/`). INFERRED: on SQLite before 3.46 the 0x10000 bit does not exist, so the growth check would see only tables queried on the same connection.

1.7 EXECUTED. Statistics live in the database file as the ordinary table `sqlite_stat1`. A store analyzed by the `sqlite3` shell, then opened by `DatabaseStorage`, served every verb with the good plan (`verbs_s56k_analyzed.txt`). First touch verified it normally, because SQLAlchemy's inspector lists 23 vfs tables and no `sqlite_*` table. An `ANALYZE` on one connection reaches a second connection that is already open at that connection's next executed statement. Before that, an `EXPLAIN` on the second connection still shows the old plan (`xconn.txt`). INFERRED: a pooled vfs connection needs no reconnect, because its next statement reloads the statistics. For `InMemoryStorage` the database is one `StaticPool` connection, so its statistics last exactly as long as its rows. EXECUTED (`memory.txt`): the 200 × 8 fan took 9,415 ms in memory with no statistics. `PRAGMA optimize=0x10012` on the one connection took 3.5 ms, and the fan then took 694 ms on the path index. After `close()` the revived store had one entry row and no `sqlite_stat1`.

## 2. How vfs uses SQLite today, and where a step could live

2.1 READ. The SQLite profile declares connection pragmas, stamped at every pool checkout, and file pragmas, run once at first touch (`src/vfs/storage/backends/database/dialects.py:389-424`, `engine.py:622-656`, `engine.py:430-437`). None of them touches statistics, and nothing in `src/` runs `ANALYZE` or `PRAGMA optimize` (EXECUTED: `grep -rni "analyze\|pragma optimize" src/`).

2.2 READ. First touch applies the file settings, probes sqlite-vec, then verifies or provisions under `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` (`engine.py:408-421`, `480-518`). Provisioning inserts the meta row, the lock row, one grants row, and the root entry `/` (`engine.py:520-547`). INFERRED: a freshly provisioned store is therefore never empty. Its entries table holds one row, which is exactly what an `ANALYZE` at provisioning would record.

2.3 READ. Every verb that writes runs through one runner, `_mutate`, which commits only when the batch succeeds (`backend.py:1774-1803`). That covers write, edit, mkdir, delete, restore, sweep, move, copy, edges, memberships, and every reindex phase: the lease claim, beat and release, the render landings, the gram build and publish, and the re-convergence repairs (`backend.py:1147-1325`). `reindex` is a verb the caller must invoke (`backend.py:1125`). `SupportsReindex` promises it is "never required for correctness", because the search verbs serve from their scan tier between builds (`src/vfs/storage/protocol.py:459-468`). `close()` disposes the engine and drops the ready latch (`engine.py:358-385`). SQLAlchemy gives an aiosqlite file URL an `AsyncAdaptedQueuePool` and `:memory:` a `StaticPool` (`../sqlalchemy/lib/sqlalchemy/dialects/sqlite/aiosqlite.py:340-347`). vfs serializes sessions on the latter (`engine.py:181-185`) and re-arms first touch when it re-mints (`engine.py:741-756`).

2.4 INFERRED. Five places are candidates. Provisioning, inside first touch. Connection open, at checkout or at first touch. Connection close, in `EngineHost.close`. A write-count threshold, kept by the host. After every committed mutation, in `_mutate`. Reindex is a sixth only in name, because its writes already pass through `_mutate`.

## 3. What the other four engines do on their own

3.1 READ. PostgreSQL's autovacuum daemon issues `ANALYZE` when the rows changed since the last analysis exceed `autovacuum_analyze_threshold + autovacuum_analyze_scale_factor × reltuples`, 50 + 10% by default (https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/routine-vacuuming.html, sections 24.1.3 and 24.1.6). Between analyses "the planner will scale the values it finds in `pg_class` to match the current physical table size" (https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/planner-stats.html). INFERRED: SQLite's planner does no such scaling, which is why stale-and-small statistics hurt here (4.4) and do not hurt there.

3.2 READ. SQL Server's `AUTO_CREATE_STATISTICS` and `AUTO_UPDATE_STATISTICS` are on by default. A statistic is refreshed when a query uses it after `MIN(500 + 0.20 n, SQRT(1000 n))` modifications, for example 44,721 at 2 million rows. Microsoft says to keep both on (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/statistics/statistics).

3.3 READ. Oracle's automatic optimizer statistics collection runs in every predefined maintenance window, one a day. It gathers objects with missing statistics, or stale ones (more than 10% modified, `STALE_PERCENT`), and Oracle "strongly recommends" leaving it on. An opt-in high-frequency task runs every 15 minutes (https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/oracle-database/23/tgsql/gathering-optimizer-statistics.html).

3.4 READ. MariaDB's InnoDB keeps persistent statistics on disk (`innodb_stats_persistent`, on by default) and recalculates them automatically when more than a tenth of a table's rows change (`innodb_stats_auto_recalc`, on by default) (https://mariadb.com/kb/en/innodb-persistent-statistics/).

3.5 INFERRED. None of the four needs vfs to act, so the step belongs to the SQLite profile alone. It is a decision SQLAlchemy takes no position on, which is the bar AGENTS.md sets for a `DialectProfile` field. Two windows exist where a server engine may plan a fresh bulk load before its automatic statistics arrive: Oracle's daily window, and PostgreSQL's autovacuum naptime. They were not measured here.

4.4 EXECUTED, stale statistics (`cost_stale.txt`, `cost_stale9x.txt`). Statistics taken at 1,037 rows, then grown 100× to 103,601 rows through vfs writes: the fan took 1,733 ms as a full SCAN, against 17.7 ms on fresh statistics. With 3, 30 or 300 entry rows and fresh statistics, the 50 × 8 fan and the 50-root glob both plan a full SCAN. At 3,000 rows they seek `ix_vfs_entries_path`. With no statistics at any of those sizes they seek `ix_vfs_entries_deleted_at`. READ: the existing plan pin `test_multi_index_or_plan_survives_the_ext_facts` asserts "no SCAN" on a two-file store (`tests/storage/database/test_reads.py:502-525`). INFERRED: that pin depends on the planner's no-statistics default. Once statistics are gathered after the seed write, it would see a correct SCAN.

4.7 EXECUTED, two alternatives on the schema side (`cost_partial100k.txt`, `cost_partial1m.txt`, `seed.txt`). Replacing `ix_vfs_entries_deleted_at` with the partial index `WHERE deleted_at IS NOT NULL` fixed the fan with no statistics at all: 17.6 ms at 103,601 rows and 26.6 ms at 1,036,001. The partial index still served `deleted_at IS NOT NULL`, `deleted_at < ?` and the trash-root query. It does not remove the dependence on statistics. With no statistics at 1,036,001 rows, the kind glob still seeked `ix_vfs_entries_kind` (5,584 ms against 2.9 ms). Under `0x10012`'s capped statistics the fan seeked `ix_vfs_entries_ext` instead (42,103 ms). Seeding statistics is the other alternative. `exp_seed.py` wrote the 23 `sqlite_stat1` rows measured on the 56,001-row store into stores of 11,201 and 1,036,001 rows, verbatim and scaled to one million, and scanned no data: 2.5 to 7.7 ms. Every captured statement then seeked `ix_vfs_entries_path` at both sizes.

## 5. Prior art

5.1 READ. SQLite's own advice is in 1.6: optimize on close for short-lived connections, on open and periodically for long-lived ones, and after schema changes.

5.2 READ. Immich's mobile app, on Drift over SQLite, runs `PRAGMA optimize` after every schema migration (`../immich/mobile/lib/data/db/main/database.dart:401`). It also runs `PRAGMA optimize=0x10002` then `PRAGMA optimize` at the start of each background upload task (`database.dart:151-160`, `../immich/mobile/lib/domain/services/background_worker.service.dart:129`, `146`, `236-242`). Failures are logged and ignored. Its server runs `VACUUM ANALYZE` on PostgreSQL after vector-index work (`../immich/server/src/repositories/database.repository.ts:252-256`, `356`).

5.3 READ. sqlite-utils never runs statistics on its own. It offers `analyze=True` on `insert_all`, `delete_where` and `create_index`, plus `db.analyze()` and an `analyze` CLI command (`../sqlite-utils/sqlite_utils/db.py:2030-2040`, `3396-3460`, `4095-4115`, `4692-5027`; `docs/python-api.rst:1108`, `1244`, `3148-3175`). Its docs say to run it "if your database is large and you do not think your indexes are being efficiently used".

5.4 READ. Datasette runs no `ANALYZE`. It hides `sqlite_stat1` through `sqlite_stat4` from its table lists (`../datasette/datasette/utils/sqlite.py:150-158`). Litestream issues neither `ANALYZE` nor `PRAGMA optimize` outside its test scripts (EXECUTED: `grep -rni`). Turso supports `ANALYZE` but not `PRAGMA optimize` (`../turso/COMPAT.md:109`, `262`).

5.5 INFERRED. The prior art splits two ways. Libraries leave statistics to the caller (sqlite-utils, datasette). Applications that own their database run SQLite's own optimize at points they control: after migrations, at the start of maintenance work. vfs is the second kind: it owns its schema, and the caller never sees SQL. None of the prior art keys the step to its own writes. The growth rule inside `PRAGMA optimize` does that keying, at negligible cost, once something calls it after each commit.

## 6. The candidates weighed

INFERRED from sections 1 to 5:

| Candidate | What it fixes | What it leaves | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside `reindex` only (the developer's idea) | stores whose owner runs reindex | glob-only and never-reindexed stores, `InMemoryStorage` fixtures, ETL ingest that never searches; reindex is optional by contract (2.3) | subsumed: reindex gets the step through `_mutate` |
| Once at provisioning | nothing | records the single root row; scans every verb (4.2) | rejected |
| On open (`0x10002` at first touch) | an existing store reopened after growth | a fresh store is analyzed at one row (2.2); a long-lived process never refreshes | not needed beside the post-commit step |
| On close | nothing reliably | close is not guaranteed; the in-memory store dies with it; the default mask is capped (4.3) | rejected |
| A vfs write counter | growth seen by this host | re-implements SQLite's rule; blind to other processes' writes | rejected for SQLite's own rule |
| Capped `PRAGMA optimize` or any `analysis_limit` | cost | bad plans above about 64 × the cap (4.3) | rejected |
| Seeded statistics at provisioning | every plan here, O(1) (4.7) | a hand-kept table per index; not shaped by real data; overwritten by any capped optimize a third party runs | a complement for a later spec, not the answer |
| Partial tombstone index | the tombstone trap with no statistics (4.7) | the `kind` and `ext` traps | a complement for a later spec |
| Post-commit `PRAGMA optimize=0x10002` in `_mutate` | every shape measured, at every size, through every writer, reindex included (4.5) | the crossing commit pays one full analysis (see 7) | recommended |

## 7. The recommendation

INFERRED. The statistics step belongs to the engine host. It runs from the shared mutation runner `_mutate` (`backend.py:1774-1803`), right after a successful commit, on its own short-lived session outside the op's transaction. A failure there never changes the committed op's Result. The statement is a SQLite-only `DialectProfile` field, for example a `statistics_refresh` statement, set to `PRAGMA optimize=0x10002` on `SQLITE` and absent on every other profile and on `GENERIC`. The four server engines maintain their own statistics (section 3). The trigger is every committed mutation, and SQLite decides whether any work follows: a table whose index lacks statistics, or whose size moved about tenfold. The step never runs at provisioning, never sets `analysis_limit`, and never uses the default mask, because both caps re-create the trap (4.3). `reindex` needs nothing of its own, because its claim, landings, publish and release all commit through `_mutate` (2.3). `InMemoryStorage` is covered by the same path on its one connection (1.7).

The cost bound at scale, INFERRED from 4.3 and 4.5: a commit that changes nothing costs one pragma, under 0.09 ms measured. A commit that crosses a growth step pays one uncapped analysis of the tables that moved, about 0.9 µs a row here. That was 5 ms at 10,000 rows, 256 ms at 450,000 and 0.94 s at 1,036,001, so roughly 10 s at 10 million rows. Crossings come about once per sevenfold to tenfold growth, so the total over a store's whole growth stays within a small constant of one full analysis at its final size. That is 0.30 s for growth to a million rows, against about 300 s to write those rows through vfs (EXECUTED: `build_s1m.txt`, 925,000 files in 300.3 s). The cost is linear, and that is an honest scale profile, not a cap. The spec must still own one consequence. The commit that crosses a step on a very large store holds SQLite's write lock for the whole analysis. Past about 5 million rows that outlasts the profile's `busy_timeout` of 5,000 ms (`dialects.py:398`), and a rival writer then sees `SQLITE_BUSY`, which vfs retries (`dialects.py:411`). One direction is to analyze table by table: the 0x1 debug bit lists the statements (EXECUTED: `PRAGMA optimize=0x10003` printed one `ANALYZE "main"."<table>"` per table due), so each table holds the lock alone. The same listing also restricts the step to vfs's own tables on a borrowed engine whose file holds other tables.

How tests pin it, by counting and never by timing. One: a store written through `storage.write` with 10,000 files has a `sqlite_stat1` row for `ix_vfs_entries_deleted_at` whose two integers are equal, which proves an uncapped analysis ran. Two: `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` of the glob fan statement recorded from that store's `storage.glob` names `ix_vfs_entries_path` in every branch and never `ix_vfs_entries_deleted_at`. The same holds for a roots glob with `kind="file"` and `ix_vfs_entries_kind`. Three: a freshly provisioned store has no `sqlite_stat1` row for any `vfs_entries` index. Four: a one-file write to an analyzed store issues exactly one statistics statement, counted through `before_cursor_execute`, and the `0x10003` listing then names no entries table. Five: the same pins pass on `InMemoryStorage`. Six: every non-SQLite profile declares no statistics statement. A capped-statistics regression can be pinned at test scale by writing a capped `sqlite_stat1` row by hand (1.4 puts the flip at 64 × the cap) and asserting the plan flips. That shows why the step must not cap. The existing "no SCAN" pin on a two-file store (4.6) must move to a store of a few thousand rows, or assert on the index it seeks instead of the absence of a scan.

## What this memo does not settle

The gram, segment and lexical tables after `reindex`: the experiment that runs `exp_verbs.py --reindex` on a 56,000-row store, with and without statistics, was blocked (opening paragraph) and is the next run once the tree imports.

The write-lock hold at 10 million rows and beyond: the experiment grows a store to 10 million entry rows, holds a rival writer during the crossing commit's analysis, and counts its `SQLITE_BUSY` retries, whole-database against table-by-table.

The server engines' windows before automatic statistics arrive: the experiment, on the engine legs, bulk-writes 10,000 files and explains the fan immediately, on PostgreSQL before autovacuum's naptime and on Oracle before its maintenance window.

SQLite older than 3.46, where the 0x10000 bit does not exist (1.6): the experiment runs the growth script of 4.5 under an interpreter linked against SQLite 3.45 and counts the re-analyses.

Whether the partial tombstone index or seeded statistics should join the step (4.7): each is a schema decision for its own spec, and the experiment is the 4.3 table rerun with each in place.

## One line

Without fresh, full statistics SQLite picks a low-cardinality index and a scoped glob slows down 25 to 2,500 times, so vfs should run `PRAGMA optimize=0x10002` after every committed SQLite mutation, never at provisioning and never capped, and let reindex inherit it rather than own it.
