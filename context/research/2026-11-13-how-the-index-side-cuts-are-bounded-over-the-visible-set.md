## The question

The law of [the grants spec](../specifications/archive/058-row-level-permission-grants/spec.md) is that a hidden row is absent and every statistic is over the visible set: nothing a caller observes may change with a row it cannot read. Three cuts on grep's and search's index side are taken over lists of surrogate entry ids before any entry row is read, so they count ids the caller cannot read. How can each cut be taken over the ids the caller may read, at a per-call cost that does not grow with the corpus, on every supported engine?

## What is known today

1. READ, vfs at `5d47a21a`. The candidate cap: `_index_doc_ids` in `src/vfs/storage/backends/database/grep.py` hands the chosen posting blobs and the scope's allow-list to the engine's candidate kernel, `candidate_ids(fed, allow, SCAN_TIER_BUDGET)` (`grep.py:453`), which intersects, meets the allow-list and keeps the lowest `SCAN_TIER_BUDGET` doc ids. The caller's view is applied only after the entry rows of the capped ids are fetched (`grep.py:277` to `:283`).
2. READ. The deferred ladder: when `_ladder_defers(chosen, meta, len(allow))` (`grep.py:445`, `:494`) prices verifying the whole allow-list below fetching the postings, `grep_rows` takes `allow[:SCAN_TIER_BUDGET]` as the candidates and `len(allow)` as the nominated count (`grep.py:262`). Both the price and the slice read the allow-list's length, which counts every entry in the scope. The gram choice (`_choose_grams`, `grep.py:484`) reads `doc_count`, which also counts every document; it changes which lawful superset is verified, and so an answer only through the cap.
3. READ. The rung test: `search_rows` in `src/vfs/storage/backends/database/search.py` takes the narrow rung when `len(allow) <= SCOPE_ID_BUDGET` (`search.py:346`), and `_vector_leg` takes the same test (`search.py:786`). The narrow rung is exact; the wide rung scores first and gates after, deepening at most once.
4. READ. A file written since the last index pass already holds segment ids, so it is in the allow-list (item 2 and item 3 count it); it holds no postings in the published epoch unless it was indexed before and has since been rewritten, in which case its old postings still feed the candidate cap (item 1) until the next publish.
5. EXECUTED for the scan-tier spec, with the cap lowered to 2: two indexed matches a caller cannot read, with lower ids than its one readable match, left that caller with no answer and a `truncated` record, where a store without them answered the match.
6. EXECUTED for the scan-tier spec, with `SCOPE_ID_BUDGET` lowered to 2 and the fusion depth to 1: three files the caller cannot read inside its glob scope moved its search from the narrow rung to the wide one; its readable in-scope file was not answered, and the window record's `found` fell from 1 to 0.
7. EXECUTED by the spec review on a build of the scan-tier spec, with the cut at 2: between two stores that differ only in five files awaiting the index that the caller cannot read, the allow-list of one scoped grep counted 4 ids on one and 9 on the other; the ladder deferred on one and not the other, and only one answer carried a `truncated` record.
8. EXECUTED for the scan-tier spec on a 20,000-file SQLite store whose every body holds one common word, with a caller reading 10 files under `/big/mine` and none of the other 19,990 under `/big`: a grep scoped to `/big/**` builds an allow-list of 20,201 ids and does not defer; pricing it over what the caller can read would need every listed id's row, 21 statements at the 1,000-element floor and 20,201 row visits, where today the price reads a list's length. A grep scoped to `/big/mine/**` builds 10 ids and defers, fetching no posting bytes; with deferral forbidden it fetched the 60,009 posting bytes of the chosen grams, about 3 bytes a document.
9. INFERRED from item 8 and [the reference deployment](../../AGENTS.md). The direct fixes are not bounded. Pricing or slicing over readable ids costs a row visit per listed id, so a scope over 2,000,000 files is about 2,000 statements a call at the 1,000-element floor. Never deferring for a partial caller fetches the common gram's postings on every narrow scoped grep, about 30 MB a call at 10,000,000 files, which the code's own cost model (`grep.py:137` to `:139`) prices near 1.65 s against about 0.75 ms to verify a 10-file scope. Capping only readable candidates needs each candidate's row, about a thousand statements a call for a common gram's millions of candidates, against about 25 today.
10. READ, the directions prior work points to. [The document-level security memo](2026-09-30-search-document-level-security-prior-art.md) records that engines make a permission filter's cost independent of the grant count by sorting the index, so that a filter on the sort key is a contiguous id range found by binary search (Lucene's index sorting), and proposes path-ordered ordinals per epoch, which turn a caller's rights into ordinal ranges (its candidate B); it records that Lucene's kNN query chooses pre-filtering or post-filtering by the filter's size (its candidate C), and that path-ordered ordinals must meet the rows written or moved after the epoch was built, which Lucene answers with a live-docs correction applied last. The grants spec deferred path-ordered lexical ids to a design of their own (its Q3, `058-row-level-permission-grants/spec.md:138`); no such design exists.

## The experiment that answers it

The research is done when each item below has a labelled result.

1. In a scratch copy of the tree, number the posting doc ids and the segment allow-list in path order within an epoch, and give the candidate kernel the caller's rights as ordinal ranges to meet before its cap. The result is whether the cap then counts only ids the caller may read, and the statements and bytes a call spends, against today, on stores of 10,000 and 100,000 files, for a caller seeing 1 %, 10 % and 50 % of the store.
2. The same for the ladder's price and slice, and for search's rung test in both legs: the count each one reads, over readable ids, at what cost.
3. The live correction: the rows written, moved or deleted since the epoch, which hold no ordinal or a stale one, counted per call and costed at the reference deployment's dirty set at this queue's cadence (a daily rebuild) and at a 15-minute pass.
4. Each experiment's two-store comparison: the observables of a caller over two stores that differ only in rows it cannot read, field for field, for grep and search, scoped and unscoped.
5. What the following spec needs from this memo: the numbering's cost on the rebuild path (the multiplier that runs about 11,000,000 visits a day), the per-call counts, and the engines on which a range meet in the kernel is not enough. The choice is that spec's.

## One line

Three index-side cuts take their count over surrogate ids before any row is read, so they count ids a caller cannot read, and the direct fixes cost a row visit per listed id; the research is to cost path-ordered ids that make a caller's rights a range the kernel can meet before it cuts.
